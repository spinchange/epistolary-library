## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXLVIII**

**Usbek to the chief eunuch, at the seraglio in Ispahan**

### Translation
Receive with this letter limitless power over the whole seraglio. Command there with as much authority as I myself do. Let fear and terror walk with you. Run from apartment to apartment, carrying punishments and chastisements. Let everything live in consternation; let everything dissolve in tears before you. Question the whole seraglio, beginning with the slaves. Spare not even what I love. Let everything submit to your dreadful tribunal. Bring the most hidden secrets to light. Purify that infamous place, and make virtue, now banished, return to it. For from this moment I place upon your head the slightest faults that may be committed there. I suspect that Zélis was the one to whom the letter you intercepted was addressed: examine that with the eyes of a lynx.

*From ***, the 11th day of the moon of Zilhagé, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson tracks this violent delegation of authority very closely. The letter is short and almost entirely imperative, and he reproduces that sequence with only small stylistic adjustments.

### Main differences
- **Scope of power**
  - French: `un pouvoir sans bornes`
  - Fresh translation: `limitless power`
  - Davidson: `unlimited power`
  - This is essentially a stylistic difference only.

- **Image of tears**
  - French: `que tout fonde en larmes devant vous`
  - Fresh translation: `let everything dissolve in tears before you`
  - Davidson: `let dismay be upon all, and tears flow from every eye in your presence`
  - Davidson clarifies the image, but the French is more sweeping and theatrical.

- **`N'épargnez pas mon amour`**
  - Fresh translation: `Spare not even what I love`
  - Davidson: `spare not my love`
  - Davidson is closer word-for-word, while the fresh translation makes explicit that Usbek means the women he cherishes.

- **The final suspicion**
  - French: `Je soupçonne Zélis d'être celle à qui la lettre ... s'adressoit`
  - Fresh translation stays very close to the syntax.
  - Davidson also keeps the point well; there is little substantive divergence here.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: nakedly authoritarian, ceremonial, and cruel.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the chain of imperatives and the accumulating atmosphere of terror.
- **Davidson**: very faithful, with only slight smoothing of imagery.

### OCR / cleanup notes
- The English clean date line reads `Itth` for `11th`, a straightforward OCR error.
- The letter body itself is otherwise comparatively clean.

## Working conclusion
Letter CXLVIII is one of the closest French/English matches in this batch. The main point of interest is not mistranslation so much as tone:
- Davidson keeps the violence intact
- but he occasionally explains images that the French leaves in a more absolute, rhetorical form
