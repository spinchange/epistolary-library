## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CX**

**Rica to [***]**

### Translation
The University of Paris is the eldest daughter of the kings of France - and very eldest indeed, for she is more than nine hundred years old; so from time to time she dreams.

I was told that some time ago she had a great quarrel with certain doctors over the letter Q, which she wanted pronounced like a K. The dispute grew so heated that some people were stripped of their property. Parliament had to settle the disagreement, and by a solemn decree it granted all the subjects of the king of France permission to pronounce that letter as they pleased. It was a fine sight to see the two most respectable bodies in Europe occupied in deciding the fate of a letter of the alphabet.

It seems to me, my dear ***, that the heads of even the greatest men grow narrower when they are assembled, and that where there are more wise men there is also less wisdom. Great corporate bodies always attach themselves so strongly to trifles, formalities, and empty customs that essentials never come until afterwards. I have heard that when a king of Aragon assembled the estates of Aragon and Catalonia, the first sessions were spent deciding in what language the deliberations would be framed. The dispute was so heated that the estates would have broken up a thousand times if someone had not invented an expedient: the proposal would be made in Catalan, and the reply given in Aragonese.

*From Paris, the 25th day of the moon of Zilhagé, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the letter's satirical target well: venerable institutions become ridiculous when collective bodies devote themselves to trivial formal disputes instead of essentials.

### Main differences
- **Dreaming vs dotage**
  - French: `elle a plus de neuf cents ans: aussi rêve-t-elle quelquefois`
  - Fresh translation keeps the lightly surreal `she dreams from time to time`.
  - Davidson's `she dotes now and again` is witty, but more interpretive.

- **The heads of great men**
  - French: `les têtes des plus grands hommes s'étrécissent lorsqu'elles sont assemblées`
  - Fresh translation preserves the striking bodily image of heads narrowing in assembly.
  - Davidson turns this into `the ablest men grow stupid`, which captures the gist but loses the metaphor.

- **Trifles before essentials**
  - French: `l'essentiel ne va jamais qu'après`
  - Fresh translation keeps the blunt ordering: essentials come only afterwards.
  - Davidson is close, but slightly smoother.

- **Language of deliberation**
  - Fresh translation keeps the bureaucratic absurdity of deciding in what language deliberations will be framed.
  - Davidson remains effective here, though the cadence is somewhat more polished.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: concise, dry, and amused by institutional pedantry.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the humor clipped and the corporate satire explicit.
- **Davidson**: readable and witty, but occasionally more idiomatic than literal.

### OCR / cleanup notes

## Working conclusion
Letter CX is short but precise. Davidson captures its ridicule of institutional solemnity, while a fresh translation better preserves the bodily and administrative images through which Montesquieu makes the satire bite.
