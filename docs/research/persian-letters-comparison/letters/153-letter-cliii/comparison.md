## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CLIII**

**Usbek to Solim, at the seraglio in Ispahan**

### Translation
I place steel in your hand. I entrust to you what I now have dearest in the world: my vengeance. Enter upon this new office, but bring to it neither heart nor pity. I am writing to my wives to obey you blindly; in the confusion of so many crimes, they will fall before your gaze. I must owe my happiness and my repose to you: give me back my seraglio as I left it. But begin by making it atone for itself. Exterminate the guilty, and make those tremble who intended to become so. What may you not hope from your master for services so signal? It will depend only on you to raise yourself above your very condition, and above all the rewards you have ever desired.

*Paris, the 4th day of the moon of Chahban, 1719.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson reproduces the broad command faithfully. The letter is a concentrated authorization of terror, and he preserves both its imperatives and its promise of reward.

### Main differences
- **`Je te mets le fer à la main`**
  - Fresh translation: `I place steel in your hand`
  - Davidson: `I place the sword in your hand`
  - Davidson makes the weapon explicit; the French `fer` is slightly more general and more brutal in its abstraction.

- **Heart and pity**
  - French: `n'y porte ni coeur ni pitié`
  - Fresh translation: `bring to it neither heart nor pity`
  - Davidson: `banish all feeling, all pity`
  - Davidson is effective, but the French more literally forbids bringing any heart into the office at all.

- **`Commence par l'expier`**
  - Fresh translation: `begin by making it atone for itself`
  - Davidson: `Begin by purifying it`
  - This is the most important difference in the letter. `Expier` is harsher and more punitive than `purify`.

- **Potential guilt**
  - French: `ceux qui se proposoient de le devenir`
  - Fresh translation: `those who intended to become so`
  - Davidson: `those ... who are inclined to become so`
  - Davidson slightly softens deliberate intention into inclination.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: cold, vengeful, and administratively murderous.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the letter's clipped brutality and its promise of advancement through repression.
- **Davidson**: close in sense, but a little less severe in the crucial `expier` line.

### OCR / cleanup notes
- The English clean file still carries a footnote intrusion in the middle of `Begin by purifying 1 it`.
- That corruption does not obscure the general sense, but it is a good reminder to consult the raw excerpt in disputed places.

## Working conclusion
Letter CLIII is broadly faithful in Davidson, but one lexical choice matters a great deal:
- `expier` is not just `purify`
- it frames the seraglio as something that must pay for its crimes
- and that nuance sharpens the letter's logic of exemplary vengeance
