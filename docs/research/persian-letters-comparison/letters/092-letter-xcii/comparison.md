## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XCII**

**Usbek to Rustan, at Ispahan**

### Translation
There is here a person disguised as a Persian ambassador who insolently makes a plaything of the two greatest kings in the world. He brings gifts to the monarch of the French that ours could not offer even to a king of Irimette or Georgia; and by his cowardly avarice he has stained the majesty of the two empires.

He has made himself ridiculous before a people who claim to be the most polished in Europe, and he has caused it to be said in the West that the king of kings rules only over barbarians.

He has received honors that he seemed to want refused to him; and, as if the court of France cared more for Persian grandeur than he did, it made him appear with dignity before a people by whom he is despised.

Do not repeat this at Ispahan. Spare the head of an unhappy man. I do not want our ministers to punish him for their own imprudence and for the unworthy choice they made.

*Paris, the last day of the moon of Gemmadi II, 1715.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson keeps the satirical thrust of the letter: the false ambassador disgraces both Persia and France by turning diplomacy into shabby theater.

### Main differences
- **`travesti en ambassadeur`**
  - Fresh translation: `disguised as a Persian ambassador`
  - Davidson: `burlesques the part of Persian ambassador`
  - Davidson is vivid, but the French is more plainly about masquerade.

- **`lâche avarice`**
  - Fresh translation keeps `cowardly avarice`.
  - Davidson's `wretched avarice` weakens the moral sting.

- **Public contempt**
  - French: `un peuple dont il est le mépris`
  - Fresh translation keeps the idea that he has become the people's object of contempt.
  - Davidson's `a people who scorn him` is close, but less cutting.

- **Ministerial blame**
  - Davidson is close in the final sentence.
  - The French is especially pointed in making ministerial bad judgment the deeper cause of the scandal.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: brief, cutting, and politically embarrassed.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the insult direct and compact.
- **Davidson**: readable, though slightly more theatrical than the French.

### OCR / cleanup notes

## Working conclusion
Davidson is broadly sound, but this short letter benefits from a more literal treatment of the diplomatic masquerade and the humiliating force of `lâche avarice`.
