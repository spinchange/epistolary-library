## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter IX**

**The chief eunuch to Ibbi, at Erzurum**

### Translation
You follow your former master on his travels; you cross provinces and kingdoms; grief cannot make much impression on you; every moment shows you something new; everything you see refreshes you and makes time pass without your feeling it.

It is not the same with me. Shut up in a dreadful prison, I am always surrounded by the same objects and devoured by the same sorrows. I groan, crushed under the burden of fifty years of cares and anxieties; and in the course of a long life I cannot say I have had a single serene day or a single tranquil moment.

When my first master formed the cruel project of entrusting his wives to me, and by seductions supported with a thousand threats forced me to separate myself from myself forever, weary of serving in the harshest posts, I thought I would sacrifice my passions to my repose and my fortune. Unhappy man that I was! My occupied mind showed me the compensation, not the loss. I hoped that, unable to satisfy love, I should be rid of its attacks. Alas! they extinguished in me the effect of the passions without extinguishing their cause; and, far from relieving me, they left me surrounded by objects that incessantly irritated them. I entered the seraglio, where everything inspired regret for what I had lost. I felt myself aroused at every instant. A thousand natural graces seemed to reveal themselves to my eyes only to afflict me; and, to crown my misfortune, I always had before me a happy man. In that troubled time, I never led a woman to my master's bed, I never undressed her, without returning home with rage in my heart and dreadful despair in my soul.

That is how I passed my miserable youth. I had no confidant but myself. Laden with tedium and sorrow, I had to swallow them. Those same women I was tempted to look on with such tenderness I viewed only with severity: I would have been lost if they had penetrated me. What advantage they would have taken from it!

I remember that one day, while I was putting a woman into the bath, I felt myself so transported that I completely lost my reason and dared lay my hand in a forbidden place. At my first reflection, I thought that day would be my last. Yet I was lucky enough to escape a thousand deaths; but the beauty whom I made the confidante of my weakness sold me her silence dearly. I entirely lost my authority over her, and afterward she obliged me to compliances that exposed me a thousand times to losing my life.

At last the fires of youth passed. I am old, and in that respect I find myself in a tranquil state. I look at women with indifference, and repay them all their contempt and all the torments they made me suffer. I always remember that I was born to command them, and it seems to me that I become a man again whenever I still command them. I hate them now that I see them coldly and my reason lets me perceive all their weaknesses. Although I guard them for another, the pleasure of making myself obeyed gives me a secret joy. When I deprive them of everything, it seems to me that it is for myself, and I always receive an indirect satisfaction from it. In the seraglio I am as in a little empire, and my ambition, the only passion left to me, is a little satisfied. I see with pleasure that everything depends on me and that at every moment I am necessary. I willingly take on the hatred of all these women, which strengthens me in my post. Nor do they deal with an ingrate: I place myself in the way of all their most innocent pleasures; I always present myself to them as an immovable barrier. They form plans, and I stop them at once. I arm myself with refusals; I bristle with scruples. I never have in my mouth anything but the words duty, virtue, modesty, decency. I drive them to despair by speaking constantly of the weakness of their sex and the master's authority. Then I complain of being obliged to so much severity, and seem to suggest that I have no motive but their own interest and a great attachment to them.

That is not to say I do not, in my turn, have an infinity of displeasures, or that every day these vindictive women do not try to outdo the wrongs I do them. Their reprisals are terrible. Between us there is a kind of ebb and flow of power and submission. They always make the most humiliating tasks fall on me. They affect an unprecedented contempt, and, with no regard for my old age, make me get up at night ten times for the smallest trifle. I am endlessly crushed by orders, commissions, duties, caprices. It seems they relieve one another in exercising me, and that one fantasy succeeds another. Often they delight in making me redouble my vigilance; they feed me false confidences. One time I am told a young man has appeared around the walls, another that a noise was heard, or that a letter must be delivered. All this throws me into confusion, and they laugh at that confusion. They are delighted to see me torment myself. At other times they keep me fastened behind a door and chain me there day and night. They know well how to feign illnesses, fainting fits, terrors; they never lack a pretext to drive me where they want me. In such moments, blind obedience and boundless compliance are required. A refusal in the mouth of a man like me would be unheard of, and if I hesitated to obey them they would have the right to punish me. I would almost rather lose my life, dear Ibbi, than descend to that humiliation.

That is not all. I am never certain, for a single instant, of my master's favor. I have as many enemies in his heart as there are women there who think only of ruining me. They have quarter-hours when I am not listened to, quarter-hours when nothing is refused them, quarter-hours when I am always in the wrong. I lead irritated women into my master's bed. Do you think they work there on my behalf, and that my side is the stronger? I have everything to fear from their tears, their sighs, their embraces, and even their pleasures. They are in the place of their triumphs. Their charms become terrible to me. Present services erase all my past services in an instant, and nothing can answer for a master who is no longer his own.

How many times have I lain down in favor and risen in disgrace! On the day when I was whipped so shamefully around the seraglio, what had I done? I left a woman in my master's arms. As soon as she saw him inflamed, she poured out a torrent of tears; she complained, and managed her complaints so well that they increased in proportion to the love she aroused. How could I have defended myself at so critical a moment? I was lost when I least expected it. I was the victim of a love negotiation, of a treaty made by sighs. That, dear Ibbi, is the cruel condition in which I have always lived.

How happy you are! Your cares are limited to the person of Usbek alone. It is easy for you to please him and to remain in his favor to the last day of your life.

*From the seraglio of Isfahan, the last day of the moon of Saphar, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the large architecture of the letter very well: mutilation, frustrated desire, bureaucratic domination, retaliation by the women, and radical dependence on the master's favor. The strongest divergences come where he turns compressed, violent French into smoother Victorian narration.

### Main differences
- **`me séparer pour jamais de moi-même`**
  - Fresh translation: `force me to separate myself from myself forever`
  - Davidson: `separate myself for ever from my manhood`
  - Davidson makes explicit what the French leaves in a more shocking and metaphysical form.

- **Cause vs effect of passion**
  - French: `on éteignit en moi l'effet des passions, sans en éteindre la cause`
  - Fresh translation keeps the analytical contrast between effect and cause.
  - Davidson's `passion had been rendered inefficient` is serviceable, but less pointed.

- **Recovered manhood through command**
  - French: `il me semble que je redeviens homme`
  - Fresh translation: `it seems to me that I become a man again`
  - Davidson: `I feel as if I had recovered my lost manhood`
  - Davidson clarifies, but also heightens a phrase whose brutality lies partly in its bluntness.

- **`je me hérisse de scrupules`**
  - Fresh translation: `I bristle with scruples`
  - Davidson keeps the image, but the French context is even more aggressively performative: the eunuch weaponizes moral vocabulary.

- **`négociation amoureuse`**
  - Fresh translation: `a love negotiation`
  - Davidson: `an amorous intrigue`
  - `Négociation` is colder and more transactional than `intrigue`.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: bitter, lucid, obscene in the precise sense, and politically revealing; the eunuch is at once victim and tyrant.
- **Fresh translation**: tries to preserve the letter's shifting mixture of humiliation, hatred, and administrative pride.
- **Davidson**: effective at the narrative level, but often more literary and slightly less brutal in the letter's most disturbing turns.

### Notable OCR / edition notes

## Working conclusion
Letter IX is one of the early letters where the difference between broad fidelity and tonal fidelity matters a great deal. Davidson gets the plot of feeling right, but the French is harsher, stranger, and more conceptually exact.
