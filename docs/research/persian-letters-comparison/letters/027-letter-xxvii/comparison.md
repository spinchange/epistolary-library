## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XXVII**

**Usbek to Nessir, in Isfahan**

### Translation
We are now in Paris, that superb rival of the city of the sun.

When I left Smyrna, I charged my friend Ibben to send you a box containing a few presents for you. You will receive this letter by the same route. Though I am five or six hundred leagues away from him, I send him my news and receive his as easily as if he were in Isfahan and I in Qom. I send my letters to Marseille, from which ships continually leave for Smyrna. From there he sends those for Persia by the Armenian caravans that depart every day for Isfahan.

Rica enjoys perfect health. The strength of his constitution, his youth, and his natural gaiety place him above every trial.

But as for me, I am not well. My body and my mind are both cast down. I give myself over to reflections that grow sadder every day. My weakening health turns me toward my homeland and makes this country more foreign to me.

But, dear Nessir, I beg you: make sure my wives know nothing of the state I am in. If they love me, I want to spare them tears; and if they do not love me, I do not want to increase their boldness.

If my eunuchs thought me in danger, if they could hope for the impunity of cowardly compliance, they would soon cease to be deaf to the flattering voice of that sex whose voice makes itself heard by rocks and moves inanimate things.

Farewell, Nessir. It gives me pleasure to give you these proofs of my confidence.

*From Paris, the 5th day of the moon of Chahban, 1712.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson conveys the practical news, personal decline, and renewed jealousy of the letter effectively. He follows the structure closely, and the differences are mainly in lexical weight and in the emotional shading of Usbek's self-presentation.

### Main differences
- **Exchange of news**
  - French: `je lui donne de mes nouvelles, et je reçois des siennes`
  - Fresh translation: `I send him my news and receive his`
  - Davidson: `we exchange news`
  - Davidson is accurate in gist, but the French keeps the action more concretely in Usbek's own voice.

- **More foreign**
  - French: `me rend ce pays-ci plus étranger`
  - Fresh translation: `makes this country more foreign to me`
  - Davidson: `adds to the strangeness of this one`
  - Davidson is elegant, but the French is more personal and alienated.

- **Increase their boldness**
  - French: `je ne veux point augmenter leur hardiesse`
  - Fresh translation: `I do not want to increase their boldness`
  - Davidson: `I have no desire to increase their frowardness`
  - `Frowardness` is period-colored and slightly moralizing; `hardiesse` is closer to audacity or emboldening.

- **Cowardly compliance**
  - French: `une lâche complaisance`
  - Fresh translation keeps the moral insult.
  - Davidson's `a base compliance` is close, though a touch more formal.

- **Voice that moves rocks**
  - French: `ce sexe qui se fait entendre aux rochers et remue les choses inanimées`
  - Fresh translation preserves the grandiose image.
  - Davidson's `which can melt rocks, and move inanimate things` is vivid, but it makes the line sound more proverbial than the French.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: practical on the surface, but shadowed by illness, homesickness, and possessive anxiety.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the way ordinary travel logistics give way to emotional vulnerability and surveillance.
- **Davidson**: broadly faithful, with only minor softening and a somewhat more literary diction.
