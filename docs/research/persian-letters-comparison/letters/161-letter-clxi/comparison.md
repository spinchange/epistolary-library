## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CLXI**

**Roxane to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Yes, I have deceived you; I have seduced your eunuchs; I have toyed with your jealousy; and I knew how to make your dreadful seraglio a place of delights and pleasures.

I am going to die; poison is about to flow through my veins: for what would I do here, now that the only man who kept me attached to life is no more? I die; but my shade takes flight well accompanied: I have just sent ahead of me those sacrilegious guards who spilled the finest blood in the world.

How could you think me credulous enough to imagine that I was in the world only to adore your caprices? that while you allowed yourself everything, you had the right to afflict all my desires? No: I may have lived in servitude, but I have always been free: I refashioned your laws by the laws of nature, and my spirit always remained independent.

You ought rather still to thank me for the sacrifice I made to you; for having lowered myself so far as to seem faithful to you; for having cowardly kept in my heart what I ought to have made known to the whole earth; finally, for having profaned virtue by allowing my submission to your whims to be called by that name.

You were astonished not to find in me the transports of love: if you had known me well, you would have found in me all the violence of hatred.

But for a long time you had the advantage of believing that a heart like mine was subject to you. We were both happy; you thought me deceived, and I was deceiving you.

No doubt this language seems new to you. Could it be that, after overwhelming you with grief, I should still force you to admire my courage? But it is over: poison is consuming me, my strength is abandoning me, the pen is falling from my hands; I feel even my hatred growing weak; I am dying.

*From the seraglio in Ispahan, the 8th day of the moon of Rebiab 1, 1720.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson's version is powerful and generally faithful. He preserves Roxane's defiance, her revelation of deception, her insistence on inward freedom, and the extraordinary dying cadence of the final paragraph.

### Main differences
- **Toying with jealousy**
  - French: `je me suis jouée de ta jalousie`
  - Fresh translation: `I have toyed with your jealousy`
  - Davidson: `I have made sport of your jealousy`
  - Davidson is close here; the difference is mainly tonal.

- **The departing shade**
  - French: `mon ombre s'envole bien accompagnée`
  - Fresh translation: `my shade takes flight well accompanied`
  - Davidson: `my spirit shall not pass unaccompanied`
  - Davidson keeps the sense, but the French image is more vivid and more theatrical.

- **The blood that was shed**
  - French: `le plus beau sang du monde`
  - Fresh translation: `the finest blood in the world`
  - Davidson: `the sweetest blood in the world`
  - `Sweetest` is more sentimental; `beau` is literally `beautiful` and points more toward value and excellence.

- **Freedom in servitude**
  - French: `j'ai pu vivre dans la servitude; mais j'ai toujours été libre`
  - Fresh translation: `I may have lived in servitude, but I have always been free`
  - Davidson: `I have lived in slavery, and yet always retained my freedom`
  - Davidson is strong and close here.

- **The crucial middle paragraph**
  - French: `tu as eu longtemps l'avantage de croire qu'un coeur comme le mien t'étoit soumis. Nous étions tous deux heureux; tu me croyois trompée, et je te trompois`
  - Fresh translation keeps the sequence of belief, mutual happiness, and deception.
  - Davidson: `you have had the satisfaction of believing that you had conquered a heart like mine: now we are both delighted: you thought me deceived, and I have deceived you`
  - Davidson substantially reshapes the passage. He introduces `conquered`, changes the tense structure, and shifts the more poised `we were both happy` into the sharper `now we are both delighted`.

- **The end formula**
  - French: `Mais c'en est fait`
  - Fresh translation: `But it is over`
  - Davidson: `But all is ended now`
  - Davidson is close, though slightly more elevated.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: triumphant, lucid, accusatory, and dying all at once; Roxane's freedom is asserted most absolutely at the moment of death.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to preserve the cold clarity of her self-vindication and the physical collapse that interrupts it.
- **Davidson**: powerful and memorable, but at one key point he heightens and reshapes Roxane's rhetoric rather than simply carrying it over.

## Working conclusion
Letter CLXI is one of Davidson's stronger and more memorable renderings, but it still contains an important interpretive shift in Roxane's penultimate self-assertion. The comparison suggests:
- strong fidelity in the opening confession and closing death scene
- good preservation of Roxane's claim to inward freedom
- but a significant reshaping of `We were both happy; you thought me deceived, and I was deceiving you`

That altered middle sentence matters because it changes the emotional poise of Roxane's final triumph.
