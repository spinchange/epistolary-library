## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XXXIX**

**Hagi Ibbi to the Jew Ben Josué, a Mohammedan proselyte, at Smyrna**

### Translation
It seems to me, Ben Josué, that there are always dazzling signs that prepare for the birth of extraordinary men, as though nature were undergoing a kind of crisis, and heavenly power could bring forth only with effort.

Nothing is so marvelous as the birth of Mohammed. God, who by the decrees of his providence had resolved from the beginning to send to men that great prophet to chain Satan, created a light two thousand years before Adam which, passing from chosen one to chosen one, from ancestor to ancestor of Mohammed, finally reached him as an authentic testimony that he was descended from the patriarchs.

It was also because of this same prophet that God willed that no child should ever be conceived unless the nature of woman ceased to be unclean, and the virile member was given over to circumcision.

He came into the world circumcised, and joy appeared on his face from birth; the earth trembled three times, as if it had itself given birth; all the idols prostrated themselves; the thrones of kings were overturned; Lucifer was cast into the bottom of the sea; and it was only after swimming for forty days that he emerged from the abyss and fled to Mount Cabès, from which, in a terrible voice, he called out to the angels.

That night, God set a boundary between man and woman that neither could cross. The art of magicians and necromancers lost its power. A voice was heard from heaven speaking these words: `I have sent my faithful friend into the world.'

According to the testimony of Isben Aben, the races of birds, clouds, winds, and all the squadrons of angels gathered together to raise this child and disputed over that advantage. The birds said in their chirping that it was more fitting for them to raise him, because they could more easily gather several fruits for him from different places. The winds murmured and said: `It belongs rather to us, because we can bring him the sweetest scents from every region.' `No, no,' said the clouds, `no; he will be entrusted to our care, because at every moment we will share with him the coolness of the waters.' At this the indignant angels cried out: `What then will be left for us to do?' But a voice from heaven was heard, which ended all the disputes: `He will not be taken from the hands of mortals, because blessed are the breasts that will nurse him, and the hands that will touch him, and the house he will dwell in, and the bed on which he will rest.'

After so many striking testimonies, my dear Josué, one must have a heart of iron not to believe his holy law. What more could heaven have done to authorize his divine mission, short of overturning nature and causing the very men it wished to convince to perish?

*From Paris, the 20th day of the moon of Rhégeb, 1713.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson captures the sequence of marvels and the mock-hagiographic tone of the letter quite well. Still, his diction is often a little more solemn and orthodox than the French, whose exaggerations are funniest when rendered with complete literal seriousness.

### Main differences
- **Muhammad's mission is to `chain` Satan**
  - French: `envoyer ... ce grand prophète pour enchaîner Satan`
  - Fresh translation: `send ... that great prophet to chain Satan`
  - Davidson: `send to men that great prophet for the overthrow of Satan`
  - `Overthrow` broadens the image; the French is more concrete and vivid.

- **The providential light**
  - French: `comme un témoignage authentique`
  - Fresh translation: `as an authentic testimony`
  - Davidson: `as an authentic sign`
  - `Sign` is workable, but `testimony` better preserves the almost juridical flavor of the French.

- **The strange sentence on conception and circumcision**
  - French: `que la nature de la femme ne cessât d'être immonde, et que le membre viril ne fût livré à la circoncision`
  - Fresh translation stays close to the odd and emphatic structure.
  - Davidson simplifies the syntax into `that women should cease to be unclean, and that man should be circumcised`.
  - Davidson makes the sentence smoother, but the French's bizarre bodily literalness is part of the effect.

- **A `boundary` between man and woman**
  - French: `Dieu posa un terme entre l'homme et la femme`
  - Fresh translation: `God set a boundary between man and woman`
  - Davidson: `God placed a barrier`
  - Davidson is close, though `boundary` keeps the simpler and slightly more formal French noun.

- **Birds, clouds, winds, and angelic `squadrons`**
  - French: `les générations des oiseaux, des nuées, des vents, et tous les escadrons des anges`
  - Fresh translation keeps the expansive catalog and `squadrons`.
  - Davidson condenses this into `all the birds, the clouds, the winds, and the hosts of angels`.
  - Davidson is readable, but a little less strange and martial.

- **Authorizing the mission**
  - French: `autoriser sa mission divine`
  - Fresh translation: `authorize his divine mission`
  - Davidson: `authorize his divine mission`
  - This is one of Davidson's strongest literal matches.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: hyperbolic, mock-devout, and deliberately extravagant in its accumulation of wonders.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the straight-faced absurdity and the bodily concreteness of the miraculous details.
- **Davidson**: often good, but a bit smoother and more reverential than the French satire works best with.

## Working conclusion
Letter XXXIX is one of the easier letters to compare because Davidson remains close in overall shape. The main gain of a fresh translation is tonal: the original's extravagance is sharpest when the miraculous claims are rendered plainly and without added solemnity.
