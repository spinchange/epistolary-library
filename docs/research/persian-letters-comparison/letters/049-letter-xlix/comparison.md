## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XLIX**

**Rica to Usbek**

### Translation
The other day, while I was in my room, I saw an extraordinarily dressed dervish come in. His beard fell to his rope belt; he had bare feet; his robe was gray, coarse, and pointed in places. The whole figure struck me as so bizarre that my first thought was to send for a painter and have him made into a fantasy.

He began with a great compliment, in which he informed me that he was a man of merit and, moreover, a Capuchin. I am told, sir, he added, that you will soon return to the court of Persia, where you hold distinguished rank. I have come to ask for your protection and to beg you to obtain from the king a small dwelling near Qazvin for two or three of our religious. My father, I replied, then you mean to go to Persia? I? sir, he said. I should be very careful not to. I am provincial here, and I would not trade my condition for that of all the Capuchins in the world. What the devil are you asking me for, then? I asked. It is this, he answered: if we had that hospice, our fathers in Italy would send two or three of their religious there. You know those religious, I suppose? I said. No, sir, I do not know them. Good God! I answered. What does it matter to you, then, that they go to Persia? A fine project indeed, to send two Capuchins to breathe the air of Qazvin. That will be very useful to both Europe and Asia. It is highly necessary to involve monarchs in such a business. So this is what is called founding splendid colonies! Go on — you and your kind were not made to be transplanted, and you will do well to keep crawling in the places where you were bred.

*At Paris, the 15th day of the moon of Rhamazan, 1713.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson gives a clear and amusing version of Rica's exchange with the Capuchin. The request for a hospice near Qazvin and Rica's contemptuous response both come through well.

### Main differences
- **Painter / fantasy image**
  - French: `pour en faire une fantaisie`
  - Fresh translation: `have him made into a fantasy`
  - Davidson: `make a sketch of it`
  - Davidson loses the more whimsical sense of a fanciful genre-piece.
- **Qazvin / Casbin**
  - Fresh translation modernizes the place-name to `Qazvin`.
  - Davidson keeps the older `Casbin`.
- **Transplant / crawl**
  - French: `n'êtes point faits pour être transplantés`
  - Fresh translation keeps the horticultural metaphor.
  - Davidson also keeps it broadly, though with slightly softer phrasing.
- **Colonies satire**
  - French: `voilà ce qui s'appelle de belles colonies`
  - Fresh translation keeps the sarcasm pointed and compact.
  - Davidson's `admirable colonies` is close but a touch more mannered.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: brisk, mocking, and impatient.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps Rica's snap and the comic disproportion of the monk's request.
- **Davidson**: faithful and readable, though slightly more polished in the insults.

## Working conclusion
Letter XLIX is a short satire on missionary vanity and institutional self-importance. Davidson tracks it well; the main differences are tonal and transliterational rather than structural.
