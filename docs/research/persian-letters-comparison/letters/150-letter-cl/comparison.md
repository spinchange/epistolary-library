## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CL**

**Usbek to Narsit, at the seraglio in Ispahan**

### Translation
Wretch that you are, you have in your hands letters containing prompt and violent orders; the slightest delay can drive me to despair, and yet you remain calm under a vain pretext!

Horrible things are happening. Perhaps half my slaves deserve death. I am sending you the letter that the chief eunuch wrote to me about this before he died. If you had opened the packet addressed to him, you would have found bloody orders in it. Read those orders, then: you shall perish if you do not carry them out.

*From ***, the 25th day of the moon of Chalval, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the violence and urgency of this reprimand well. The letter is essentially a shouted command, and his English keeps that imperative pressure.

### Main differences
- **Opening insult**
  - French: `Malheureux que vous êtes`
  - Fresh translation: `Wretch that you are`
  - Davidson: `Miserable slave!`
  - Davidson intensifies and specifies the insult by adding `slave`, which is not in the French wording here.

- **Violence of the orders**
  - French: `des ordres prompts et violents`
  - Fresh translation: `prompt and violent orders`
  - Davidson: `orders for prompt and strong measures`
  - `Strong measures` is less brutal than `violent orders`.

- **The rebuke for delay**
  - French: `vous demeurez tranquille sous un vain prétexte`
  - Fresh translation keeps the accusation of calm passivity under an empty excuse.
  - Davidson is close, but a little more idiomatic and less cutting.

- **Final imperative**
  - French: `Lisez-les donc ces ordres`
  - Fresh translation: `Read those orders, then`
  - Davidson folds the line into smoother English syntax.
  - The French is more abrupt and contemptuous.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: furious, compressed, and openly murderous.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the hard edge of `violent`, `bloody`, and `you shall perish`.
- **Davidson**: still forceful, but slightly more polished and less raw.

## Working conclusion
Letter CL is brief but revealing:
- Davidson's sense is accurate
- but he slightly moderates the letter's violence through phrases like `strong measures`
- and he adds a specificity (`slave`) that the French does not explicitly use in the opening insult
