## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CLVI**

**Roxane to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Horror, night, and terror reign in the seraglio; dreadful mourning surrounds it: a tiger there vents all his rage at every moment. He has put two white eunuchs to torture, and they confessed nothing but their innocence; he has sold part of our slaves, and has forced us to exchange among ourselves those who remained to us. Zachi and Zelis received, in their rooms, in the darkness of night, treatment unworthy of them; the sacrilegious wretch did not fear to lay his vile hands upon them. He keeps each of us shut up in her own apartment; and although we are there alone, he makes us live there veiled; we are no longer allowed to speak to one another; it would be a crime to write to one another; we have nothing left free but tears.

A troop of new eunuchs has entered the seraglio, where they besiege us night and day: our sleep is constantly interrupted by their feigned or genuine suspicions. What consoles me is that all this will not last long, and that these sufferings will end with my life: it will not be a long one, cruel Usbek! I shall not give you time to put an end to all these outrages.

*From the seraglio in Ispahan, the 2nd day of the moon of Maharram, 1720.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson conveys the main facts and the urgent emotional force of the letter well. The atmosphere of collective terror is intact, and Roxane's closing turn toward death is clearly rendered.

### Main differences
- **Opening triad and surrounding grief**
  - French: `L'horreur, la nuit et l'épouvante règnent dans le sérail; un deuil affreux l'environne`
  - Fresh translation: `Horror, night, and terror reign in the seraglio; dreadful mourning surrounds it`
  - Davidson: `Horror, darkness, and terror reign in the seraglio; a dreadful affliction is upon us`
  - Davidson keeps the force of the opening, but shifts from grief surrounding the seraglio to an affliction resting on the women.

- **The remaining slaves**
  - French: `nous a obligées de changer entre nous celles qui nous restoient`
  - Fresh translation: `forced us to exchange among ourselves those who remained to us`
  - Davidson: `forced us to share among us the services of those which remained`
  - Davidson explains the practical meaning, but the French stresses compulsory rearrangement inside the household.

- **Living under the veil**
  - French: `il nous y fait vivre sous le voile`
  - Fresh translation: `he makes us live there veiled`
  - Davidson: `he forces us to wear our veils`
  - Davidson is accurate in effect, but the French is more continuous and oppressive: veiling becomes a condition of life.

- **Suspicion or mistrust**
  - French: `leurs méfiances feintes ou véritables`
  - Fresh translation: `their feigned or genuine suspicions`
  - Davidson: `their real or feigned suspicions`
  - This is very close; the difference is mostly stylistic.

- **The nearness of death**
  - French: `ces peines finiront avec ma vie: elle ne sera pas longue`
  - Fresh translation: `these sufferings will end with my life: it will not be a long one`
  - Davidson: `my troubles will end with my life. And the end is not far distant`
  - Davidson smooths the syntax, while the French binds the thought of relief directly to the shortening of Roxane's life.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: compressed, collective, and terrifying; the letter moves quickly from report to suffocation to suicidal resolve.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to preserve the speed of accumulation and the sense that every liberty has been stripped away except weeping.
- **Davidson**: faithful in broad outline, but slightly more explanatory and less stark in some of the imagery.

## Working conclusion
Letter CLVI is a strong Davidson rendering overall. The key differences are modest but consistent:
- some redistribution of imagery in the opening sentence
- a more explanatory handling of domestic arrangements in the seraglio
- a slightly less absolute rendering of the women being made to `live under the veil`
