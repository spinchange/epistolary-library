## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXII**

**Zélis to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Your daughter has reached her seventh year, and I thought it was time to move her into the inner apartments of the seraglio instead of waiting until she was ten to entrust her to the black eunuchs. One can never too early deprive a young girl of the freedoms of childhood and give her a holy education within the sacred walls where modesty dwells.

For I cannot agree with those mothers who only shut up their daughters when they are on the point of giving them a husband, who condemn them to the seraglio rather than consecrate them to it, and violently make them embrace a way of life they ought to have inspired in them. Must everything be expected from the force of reason and nothing from the gentleness of habit?

It is useless to speak to us of the subordination in which nature has placed us. It is not enough to make us feel it; we must be made to practice it, so that it may sustain us in that critical time when passions begin to be born and encourage us toward independence.

If we were attached to you only by duty, we might sometimes forget it. If we were drawn only by inclination, perhaps a stronger inclination might weaken it. But when the laws give us to one man, they steal us from all others and place us as far from them as if we were a hundred thousand leagues away.

Nature, industrious in favor of men, did not stop at giving them desires. She wanted us also to have them and to be living instruments of their happiness. She set us in the fire of the passions in order that they might live tranquilly. If they emerge from their insensibility, she destined us to lead them back into it, though we ourselves can never taste that happy state into which we put them.

Yet, Usbek, do not imagine your condition happier than mine. I have tasted here a thousand pleasures that you do not know. My imagination has labored without cease to make me understand their price. I have lived, and you have merely languished.

Even in the prison where you keep me, I am freer than you are. You cannot redouble your care to have me watched without my enjoying your anxieties. Your suspicions, your jealousy, your vexations are so many marks of your dependence.

Go on, dear Usbek. Have me watched night and day. Do not trust even the ordinary precautions. Increase my happiness while securing your own, and know that I fear only your indifference.

*From the seraglio of Isfahan, the 2nd day of the moon of Rebiab I, 1714.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson follows the whole argument well: the early confinement of a daughter, the making of female obedience into habit, and Zélis's provocative claim that the jealous husband is less free than the watched wife.

### Main differences
- **Condemn rather than consecrate**
  - French sharply opposes `condamner` and `consacrer`.
  - Fresh translation preserves that contrast.
  - Davidson keeps it broadly, though in slightly more explanatory prose.
- **Made to practice subordination**
  - French: `il faut nous la faire pratiquer`
  - Fresh translation stays close to the disciplinary force of the line.
  - Davidson softens it slightly into `learn voluntary subordination`, which adds `voluntary` not present in the French.
- **Living instruments**
  - French: `des instruments animés`
  - Fresh translation: `living instruments`
  - Davidson: `the living instruments of the enjoyment of our masters`
  - Davidson heightens the explicitness and adds `masters`.
- **I have lived / you have languished**
  - Fresh translation keeps the stark opposition.
  - Davidson also keeps it, and it remains one of the letter's strongest lines.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: coolly ideological, then sharply erotic and defiant.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to preserve the chilling lucidity of the argument without softening its sexual politics.
- **Davidson**: broadly faithful, though occasionally more explanatory and less severe.

## Working conclusion
Letter LXII is a key seraglio letter because it moves from social doctrine to psychological reversal. Davidson keeps the structure, but the fresh translation better preserves the hard coercive logic of habit and confinement.
