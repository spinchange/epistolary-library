## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXLVII**

**The chief eunuch to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Things have reached a state that can no longer be sustained: your wives have imagined that your departure left them in complete impunity. Horrible things are happening here, and I myself tremble at the cruel account I am about to give you.

A few days ago, as Zélis was going to the mosque, she let her veil fall and appeared almost with her face uncovered before all the people.

I found Zachi lying with one of her female slaves, a thing so forbidden by the laws of the seraglio.

By the greatest chance in the world I intercepted a letter, which I am sending you: I have never been able to discover to whom it was addressed.

Yesterday evening, a young boy was found in the garden of the seraglio, and he escaped over the walls.

Add to this what has not reached my knowledge; for surely you are being betrayed. I await your orders, and until the happy moment when I receive them I shall remain in a mortal state. But if you do not place all these women at my discretion, I answer for none of them, and every day I shall have equally sad news to send you.

*From the seraglio at Ispahan, the 1st day of the moon of Rhegeb, 1717.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson is very close here. He keeps the sequence of accusations intact and preserves the rapid escalation from impropriety to full panic. The letter's main force lies in compression and alarm, and Davidson generally respects both.

### Main differences
- **Opening formula**
  - French: `Les choses sont venues à un état qui ne se peut plus soutenir`
  - Fresh translation: `Things have reached a state that can no longer be sustained`
  - Davidson: `Things have come to such a pass here that it is not to be endured`
  - Davidson turns institutional breakdown into a more emotional statement of endurance.

- **Status of Zachi's companion**
  - French: `une de ses esclaves`
  - Fresh translation: `one of her female slaves`
  - Davidson: `one of her maids`
  - `Maid` softens the harsher hierarchy of the seraglio.

- **The closing threat**
  - French: `je vais être dans une situation mortelle`
  - Fresh translation: `I shall remain in a mortal state`
  - Davidson: `I shall be in a state of intolerable anxiety`
  - Davidson conveys distress, but the French is more extreme and more melodramatic.

- **Authority over the women**
  - French: `si tu ne mets toutes ces femmes à ma discrétion`
  - Fresh translation: `if you do not place all these women at my discretion`
  - Davidson: `if you do not leave all these women to my discretion`
  - Davidson is close, but the fresh translation keeps the harder sense of handing them over to the eunuch's control.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: abrupt, accusatory, and panicked.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the clipped reporting style and the final threat.
- **Davidson**: very close in effect, though sometimes a little smoother and more psychological.

## Working conclusion
Letter CXLVII is one of the tighter Davidson correspondences in this run. The main differences are tonal rather than structural:
- slightly softer social vocabulary (`maid`)
- slightly less extreme phrasing in the closing threat
- a more polished English cadence overall
