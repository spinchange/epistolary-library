## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CLI**

**Solim to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
If I kept silence any longer, I would be as guilty as all those criminals you have in the seraglio.

I was the confidant of the chief eunuch, the most faithful of your slaves. When he saw himself near his end, he had me called and said these words to me: `I am dying; but the only grief I have in leaving life is that my last looks found my master's wives guilty. May Heaven guard him from all the misfortunes I foresee! May my threatening shade, after my death, come to warn those perfidious women of their duty and still intimidate them! Here are the keys of those dreadful places; go and carry them to the oldest of the black eunuchs. But if, after my death, he lacks vigilance, remember to warn your master.` As he finished these words, he expired in my arms.

I know what he wrote to you some time before his death about the conduct of your wives. There is in the seraglio a letter that would have carried terror with it if it had been opened. The one you have written since was intercepted three leagues from here. I do not know what it is, but everything turns out unhappily.

Meanwhile your wives keep no restraint at all. Since the death of the chief eunuch, it seems that everything is permitted to them. Roxane alone has remained in duty and preserves her modesty. One sees morals corrupted every day. One no longer finds on the faces of your wives that manly and severe virtue that once reigned there. A new joy spread through those places is, in my opinion, an infallible sign of some new satisfaction. In the smallest things I notice freedoms hitherto unknown. Even among your slaves there reigns a certain indolence toward their duty and toward the observance of rules that surprises me; they no longer have that ardent zeal for your service that once seemed to animate the whole seraglio.

Your wives spent eight days in the country, at one of your most abandoned houses. It is said that the slave who looks after it has been won over, and that a day before they arrived he had two men hidden in a stone recess in the wall of the principal room, from which they came out at night when we had withdrawn. The old eunuch who is now at our head is an imbecile, to whom one can make anything be believed.

I am agitated by a vengeful anger against so many treacheries, and if Heaven should will it, for the good of your service, that you judge me capable of governing, I promise you that, if your wives were not virtuous, at least they would be faithful.

*From the seraglio at Ispahan, the 6th day of the moon of Rebiab I, 1719.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson captures the narrative and the intrigue very effectively. He preserves the dying testimony of the chief eunuch, the intercepted letters, the mounting libertinism of the seraglio, and Solim's self-promotion as the ruthless replacement.

### Main differences
- **Roxane / Roxana**
  - French: `Roxane`
  - Fresh translation keeps `Roxane`.
  - Davidson uses `Roxana`, a familiar Anglicized form.
  - This is minor, but it is still an editorial choice.

- **`Vertu mâle et sévère`**
  - Fresh translation: `that manly and severe virtue`
  - Davidson: `that stern and noble virtue`
  - Davidson smooths away the striking gendered adjective `mâle`, which matters for the letter's austere moral rhetoric.

- **New joy / new satisfaction**
  - French: `une joie nouvelle ... quelque satisfaction nouvelle`
  - Fresh translation keeps the repeated `new` structure.
  - Davidson prefers `unusual gaiety` and `some uncustomary pleasure`, which reads well but is less close to the repetition in the French.

- **The hidden men**
  - French: `un réduit de pierre qui est dans la muraille`
  - Fresh translation: `a stone recess in the wall`
  - Davidson: `a secret recess in the wall`
  - Davidson is plausible, but the French concretely specifies `stone`.

- **The chilling final promise**
  - French: `si tes femmes n'étoient pas vertueuses, au moins elles seroient fidèles`
  - Fresh translation keeps the hard contrast between virtue and mere fidelity.
  - Davidson is close here and preserves the essential menace.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: suspicious, theatrical, and sinisterly ambitious.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep both the administrative tone of the report and Solim's eagerness to turn denunciation into promotion.
- **Davidson**: strong and readable, though slightly smoother where the French is harsher or stranger.

### OCR / cleanup notes
- This is one of the cleaner English excerpts in the batch.
- The main editorial issue is not OCR corruption so much as Davidson's mild normalization of names and phrasing.

## Working conclusion
Letter CLI is important because it sharpens the politics of the seraglio crisis:
- Davidson preserves the plot well
- but the French remains a little harsher in its moral vocabulary and in Solim's opportunistic self-positioning
- especially in phrases like `vertu mâle et sévère` and the final promise of `fidelity` without virtue
