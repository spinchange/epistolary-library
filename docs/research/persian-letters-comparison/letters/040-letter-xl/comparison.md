## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XL**

**Usbek to Ibben, at Smyrna**

### Translation
As soon as a great man has died, people gather in a mosque and deliver his funeral oration, which is a speech in his praise from which one would be very hard put to decide exactly what merit the deceased had.

I would ban funeral pomp: men should be wept for at their birth, not at their death. What use are the ceremonies, and all the mournful apparatus displayed before a dying man in his last moments, even the tears of his family and the grief of his friends, except to exaggerate for him the loss he is about to suffer?

We are so blind that we do not know when we should grieve or rejoice: we have almost nothing but false sorrows and false joys.

When I see the Mogul, who every year foolishly goes to place himself in a balance and have himself weighed like an ox; when I see peoples rejoicing because that prince has become more material, that is to say less capable of governing them, I pity, Ibben, the extravagance of mankind.

*From Paris, the 20th day of the moon of Rhégeb, 1713.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the aphoristic structure of this short letter very well. The main differences are less about large omissions than about small interpretive choices that slightly smooth or moralize the French.

### Main differences
- **The funeral oration as a problem of judgment**
  - French: `on seroit bien embarrassé de décider au juste du mérite du défunt`
  - Fresh translation: `one would be very hard put to decide exactly what merit the deceased had`
  - Davidson: `it would be very difficult to gather a true estimate of the deceased`
  - Davidson is accurate, though a bit more abstract than the French's direct phrasing.

- **Birth, not death, as the proper moment for tears**
  - French: `il faut pleurer les hommes à leur naissance, et non pas à leur mort`
  - Fresh translation keeps the blunt paradox almost word for word.
  - Davidson: `Men should be bewailed at their birth and not at their death`
  - This is one of Davidson's closest and strongest renderings.

- **The loss the dying man is about to `make`**
  - French: `la perte qu'il va faire`
  - Fresh translation: `the loss he is about to suffer`
  - Davidson: `the loss he is about to sustain`
  - Both are close; the French keeps the oddly practical phrasing that the dying man is about to incur a loss.

- **False sorrows and false joys**
  - French: `de fausses tristesses ou de fausses joies`
  - Fresh translation: `false sorrows and false joys`
  - Davidson: `our mirth and our sadness are nearly always false`
  - Davidson turns paired nouns into a more general summary.

- **`Plus matériel`**
  - French: `devenu plus matériel`
  - Fresh translation: `become more material`
  - Davidson: `the increase in weight of their prince`
  - Davidson explains the point clearly, but he gives up the deliberately philosophical wordplay of the French.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: brief, epigrammatic, and bitterly philosophical.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the compact paradoxes and the abrupt movement from funerals to the Mogul.
- **Davidson**: very serviceable here, though a little more explanatory in places.

## Working conclusion
Letter XL is one of the stronger Davidson matches in this run. A fresh translation still helps, mainly by keeping the French's exact paradoxes and its strange, pointed phrase `more material` instead of converting that line into plain explanation.
