## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter I**

**Usbek to his friend Rustan, in Ispahan**

### Translation
We stayed only one day in Qom. After making our devotions at the tomb of the virgin who gave birth to twelve prophets, we set out again, and yesterday, on the twenty-fifth day since our departure from Ispahan, we arrived in Tabriz.

Rica and I are perhaps the first Persians whom the desire to know has drawn out of their own country, and who have given up the comforts of a tranquil life in order to seek wisdom through labor.

We were born in a flourishing kingdom; but we did not believe its borders were the borders of our knowledge, or that only the light of the East ought to enlighten us.

Tell me what people are saying about our journey; do not flatter me, for I do not expect many to approve of it. Address your letter to Erzerum, where I shall remain for some time. Farewell, my dear Rustan. Be assured that, wherever I may be in the world, you have a faithful friend in me.

*Tabriz, the 15th day of the moon of Saphar, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson's version is close in overall sense and sequence, and it reads fluently as late-Victorian literary English. It preserves the basic movement of the letter: departure, pursuit of wisdom, rejection of provincial limits, request for news, and affectionate farewell.

### Main differences
- **Place-name modernization / transliteration choices**
  - Fresh translation: `Qom`, `Tabriz`, `Erzerum`
  - Davidson: `Koum`, `Tauris`, `Erzeroum`
  - This is mostly editorial/transliteration drift rather than a meaning difference.

- **Day-count discrepancy**
  - French: `vingt-cinquième jour` = `twenty-fifth day`
  - Davidson: `twenty-sixth day`
  - This appears to be a real translation discrepancy, not just stylistic variation.

- **Knowledge vs wisdom phrasing**
  - French: `l'envie de savoir` is more literally `the desire to know`
  - Davidson: `the thirst for knowledge`
  - Davidson is elegant but a little more elevated and abstract than the French.

- **Labor of wisdom-seeking**
  - French: `aller chercher laborieusement la sagesse`
  - Fresh translation: `seek wisdom through labor`
  - Davidson: `the laborious search after wisdom`
  - Davidson is faithful here, just more period-styled.

- **Image of intellectual limits**
  - French: `nous n'avons pas cru que ses bornes fussent celles de nos connoissances`
  - Fresh translation keeps the stronger parallel: the kingdom's borders are not the borders of knowledge.
  - Davidson smooths this into `its boundaries should limit our knowledge`, which is accurate but a little less pointed rhetorically.

- **Oriental light**
  - French: `la lumière orientale dût seule nous éclairer`
  - Fresh translation: `only the light of the East ought to enlighten us`
  - Davidson: `the lore of the East should alone enlighten us`
  - `lumière` is literally `light`; Davidson shifts it to `lore`, which interprets rather than translates.

- **Closing sentence**
  - French emphasis: `en quelque lieu du monde où je sois, tu as un ami fidèle`
  - Fresh translation preserves the direct assurance of enduring friendship.
  - Davidson's `you have in me a faithful friend` is very close and effective.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: poised, lucid, and quietly ambitious; the philosophical posture is present from the beginning but still framed as personal correspondence.
- **Fresh translation**: aims for clear modern literary English while preserving the compact logic and self-conscious intellectual ambition of the original.
- **Davidson**: graceful and readable, but somewhat more ornate and Victorian in register.

## Working conclusion
Davidson's Letter I is broadly faithful, but it already shows the kinds of differences worth tracking across the project:
- occasional factual drift (`twenty-fifth` -> `twenty-sixth`)
- interpretive substitutions (`light` -> `lore`)
- Victorian elevation of tone
- editorialized transliteration of names and places
