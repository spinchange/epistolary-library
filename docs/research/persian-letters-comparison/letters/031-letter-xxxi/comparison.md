## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XXXI**

**Rhédi to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
I am now in Venice, my dear Usbek. One may have seen all the cities in the world and still be surprised on arriving at Venice. One will always be astonished to see a city, towers, and mosques rising from beneath the water, and to find an innumerable people in a place where there ought to be nothing but fish.

But this profane city lacks the most precious treasure in the world, that is to say, running water. It is impossible there to perform a single legal ablution. It is held in abomination by our holy prophet, who never looks upon it from the height of heaven except in anger.

Were it not for that, my dear Usbek, I should be delighted to live in a city where my mind is being formed every day. I am learning the secrets of commerce, the interests of princes, and the form of their government. I do not even neglect European superstitions. I apply myself to medicine, physics, and astronomy. I study the arts. In short, I am emerging from the clouds that covered my eyes in the land of my birth.

*From Venice, the 16th day of the moon of Chalval, 1712.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson is close in structure and sense throughout this short letter. He preserves both the comic Islamic reaction to Venice and Rhédi's sincere delight in intellectual formation. The main differences lie in the handling of a few key metaphors.

### Main differences
- **Profane city**
  - French: `cette ville profane`
  - Fresh translation: `this profane city`
  - Davidson: `this heathenish city`
  - Davidson intensifies the term. `Profane` is still condemnatory, but it is less crudely polemical than `heathenish`.

- **Living / running water**
  - French: `eau vive`
  - Fresh translation: `running water`
  - Davidson: `pure water`
  - `Eau vive` is more literally living or running water, which matters because the joke concerns ritual ablution, not just purity in the abstract.

- **My mind is being formed**
  - French: `où mon esprit se forme tous les jours`
  - Fresh translation keeps the idea of ongoing formation.
  - Davidson's `where my mind is developed every day` is close, but slightly more general and modern-sounding.

- **Clouds over the eyes**
  - French: `je sors des nuages qui couvroient mes yeux`
  - Fresh translation: `I am emerging from the clouds that covered my eyes`
  - Davidson: `I am couching my eyes of the film which covered them`
  - Davidson reaches for a medical idiom that is striking but not what the French literally says.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: brisk, amused, and unexpectedly earnest in its closing intellectual self-description.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the tension between comic religious prejudice and genuine curiosity.
- **Davidson**: faithful overall, though somewhat more ornate in the last image.

Letter XXXI is a compact but revealing comparison case. Davidson is dependable here, but the French's final metaphor of intellectual emergence is clearer and more elegant when translated more literally.
