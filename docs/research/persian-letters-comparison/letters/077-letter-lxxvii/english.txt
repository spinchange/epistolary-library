Ibben to Usbek, at Paris. 

My dear Usbek, it seems to me that, in the eyes of a 
true Mussulman, misfortunes are not so much punish¬ 
ments as warnings. Those are priceless days upon which 
we are led to atone for our offences. It is the time of 
prosperity that ought to be curtailed. To what end is 
all our impatience, but to show us that we are seeking

If a human creature is composed of two beings, and if 
the acknowledgment of the necessity of preserving their 
union is the chief mark of submission to the decrees of 
our Creator, that necessity should be made a religious 
law; and if the enforced preservation of this union will 
make men more responsible for their actions, it should
