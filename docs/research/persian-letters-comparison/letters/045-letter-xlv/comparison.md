## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XLV**

**Rica to Usbek**

### Translation
Yesterday morning, while I was still in bed, I heard my door being struck violently; it was suddenly opened — or rather forced — by a man with whom I had formed some acquaintance, and who seemed wholly beside himself.

His dress was more than merely modest; his wig, awry, had not even been combed; he had not had time to have his black doublet mended, and for that day he had given up the prudent precautions with which he usually disguised the ruinous state of his clothes.

Get up, he said to me; I need you for the whole day. I have a thousand purchases to make, and I shall be glad for them to be made with you. First we must go to the Rue Saint-Honoré and speak to a notary who is charged with selling an estate worth five hundred thousand livres; I want him to give me preference. On my way here I stopped for a moment in the Faubourg Saint-Germain, where I rented a townhouse for two thousand écus, and I hope to sign the contract today.

As soon as I was dressed — or nearly so — my man hurried me downstairs. Let us begin, he said, by buying a coach and setting up the equipage first. In fact we bought not only a coach, but a hundred thousand francs' worth of goods as well, in less than an hour. All this was done quickly, because my man haggled over nothing and never counted anything; so he did not disburse anything either. I was dreaming over the whole affair; and when I examined him, I found in him such a singular mixture of riches and poverty that I no longer knew what to believe. At last I broke the silence and, drawing him aside, said: Sir, who is to pay for all this? I am, he answered; come to my room and I shall show you immense treasures, riches envied by the greatest monarchs — though not by you, since you will always share them with me.

I followed him. We climbed to his fifth floor, and by a ladder hauled ourselves up to a sixth, which was a little room open to all the winds and containing nothing but two or three dozen earthen basins filled with different liquids. I rose very early this morning, he told me, and first did what I have done for twenty-five years: I went to inspect my work. I saw that the great day had come, the day that must make me richer than any man on earth. Do you see that vermilion liquid? It now has all the qualities philosophers require for the transmutation of metals. I drew from it those grains you see there: they are true gold by color, though still a little imperfect in weight. This secret, which Nicolas Flamel discovered but which Raymond Lully and a million others never ceased to seek, has come down to me; and today I find myself a happy adept. Heaven grant that I may use the treasures it has entrusted to me only for its glory.

I went out, and I came down — or rather flung myself down — those stairs in a fury, leaving that very rich man in his hospital. Farewell, my dear Usbek. I shall come and see you tomorrow, and, if you wish, we shall return to Paris together.

*At Paris, the last day of the moon of Rhegeb, 1713.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the anecdote's comic structure: a ragged visionary spends like a millionaire, reveals his alchemical workshop, and ends abandoned in delusion. The scene remains vivid and fast-moving.

### Main differences
- **Habillement / clothing detail**
  - French: `beaucoup plus que modeste`
  - Fresh translation: `more than merely modest`
  - Davidson: `to say the least, very homely`
  - Davidson idiomatizes the joke but loses the French's sly understatement.
- **He paid nothing**
  - French: `aussi ne déplaça-t-il pas`
  - Fresh translation: `so he did not disburse anything either`
  - Davidson: `and paid no money`
  - Davidson is accurate; the fresh version keeps more of the neat verbal turn.
- **Hospital vs garret**
  - French: `dans son hôpital`
  - Fresh translation: `in his hospital`
  - Davidson: `in his garret`
  - This is a significant interpretive shift. The French mocks him as half-mad or miserably lodged; `garret` domesticates the insult.
- **Richer than any man on earth**
  - French: `plus riche qu'homme qui soit sur la terre`
  - Fresh translation keeps the extravagant absolute.
  - Davidson's `the richest man in the whole world` is close but slightly more smoothed.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: comic, abrupt, and edged with contempt.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the pace and the ridicule while preserving the mock-grand style of the alchemist's speech.
- **Davidson**: entertaining and fluent, though sometimes more domesticated in idiom.

### Notable OCR / edition notes
- The English clean text is mostly stable here.
- Footnote residue remains around Raymond Lully in the OCR tradition, but the sense is recoverable.

## Working conclusion
Letter XLV is one of the sharper comic sketches in this run. Davidson tells it well, but `hospital` -> `garret` is an especially telling softening of Montesquieu's contemptuous close.
