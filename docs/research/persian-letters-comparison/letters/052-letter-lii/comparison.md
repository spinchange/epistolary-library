## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LII**

**Rica to Usbek**

### Translation
The other day I was in a gathering where I amused myself rather well. There were women there of every age: one of eighty, one of sixty, one of forty, and the last had a niece who might have been twenty or twenty-two. Some instinct led me toward the youngest, and she whispered to me: What do you say of my aunt, who at her age still wants admirers and tries to play the beauty? She is wrong, I told her; that is a design fit only for you.

A moment later I found myself beside her aunt, who said to me: What do you say of that woman, who is at least sixty and spent more than an hour at her toilette today? It is time lost, I answered, and one must have your charms to think of such things.

I went to that poor woman of sixty, pitying her in my soul, when she said in my ear: Is anything so ridiculous? Do you see that woman of eighty wearing flame-colored ribbons? She wants to make herself young, and succeeds only by coming close to childhood.

Good God, I said to myself, shall we never feel the ridiculousness of ourselves, only that of others? Perhaps, I then thought, it is a kind of happiness that we find consolation in other people's weaknesses.

Still, I was in the mood to amuse myself, and I said: we have climbed high enough; let us come back down, beginning with the old woman at the summit. Madam, I told the woman of eighty, you and that lady to whom I was just speaking resemble one another so much that you seem to be sisters; I do not think one of you is any older than the other. Indeed, sir, she answered, when one of us dies the other will have great cause to fear; I do not believe there are two days' difference between us.

Once I had secured that decrepit lady, I went to the woman of sixty. Madam, you must settle a wager I have made. I have bet that you and that lady there — and I pointed to the woman of forty — are the same age. In good faith, she said, I do not believe there are six months between us.

Then, still descending, I returned to the woman of forty. Madam, would you tell me whether you were joking when you called that young person your niece? You are as young as she is; indeed there is even a touch of age in her face that yours does not have, and the brilliance of your complexion — Listen, she said. I am her aunt, but her mother was at least twenty-five years older than I am; we are not even children of the same marriage. I have heard my late sister say that her daughter and I were born in the same year. I was right, then, madam, and you cannot blame me for being astonished.

My dear Usbek, women who feel themselves ending in advance through the loss of their charms would like to move backward toward youth. And how should they not try to deceive others, when they make every effort to deceive themselves and steal away from the most afflicting of all ideas?

*At Paris, the 3rd day of the moon of Chalval, 1713.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson keeps the comic stair-step structure of the scene very well: each woman mocks the older one above her, then claims equality with the younger one below. The closing reflection on self-deception is also preserved.

### Main differences
- **Play the beauty**
  - French: `fait la jolie`
  - Fresh translation: `tries to play the beauty`
  - Davidson: `tries to pass for a beauty`
  - Davidson is good, but slightly more idiomatic than literal.
- **Come back down**
  - French: `descendons à présent`
  - Fresh translation keeps the vertical joke explicit.
  - Davidson handles the image well too.
- **Ending in advance**
  - French: `se sentent finir d'avance`
  - Fresh translation keeps the sense of prematurely feeling their own ending.
  - Davidson's `ageing them before their time` is interpretive and a little gentler.
- **Steal away from an idea**
  - French: `se dérober à ... l'idée`
  - Fresh translation keeps the psychological evasion.
  - Davidson smooths it into a more generalized formulation.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: playful, cruel, and compactly philosophical at the close.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to preserve the nested comic geometry of the scene.
- **Davidson**: very readable and generally close, with only mild smoothing.
