## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LIX**

**Rica to Usbek**

### Translation
The other day I was in a house where there was a circle of all sorts of people. I found the conversation occupied by two old women who had vainly worked all morning to make themselves younger. One of them said: It must be admitted that the men of today are very different from those we saw in our youth: they were polite, gracious, obliging; but now I find them insufferably brutal. Everything has changed, said a man who seemed crushed by gout. Forty years ago the times were not like this: everyone was healthy, people walked about, they were cheerful, they asked only to laugh and dance; now everyone is of an intolerable sadness.

A moment later the conversation turned to politics. Good God! said an old lord, the state is no longer governed. Find me now a minister like Monsieur Colbert. I knew Monsieur Colbert very well; he was one of my friends; he always had my pensions paid before anyone else's. What beautiful order there was in the finances! Everyone was at ease; now I am ruined. Sir, said an ecclesiastic, you are speaking of the most miraculous age of our invincible monarch. Was anything greater than what he did then to destroy heresy? And do you count the abolition of duels for nothing? added another man with satisfaction, who had not yet spoken. The remark is judicious, someone whispered in my ear; that man is delighted with the edict, and observes it so well that six months ago he took a hundred blows with a stick rather than violate it.

It seems to me, Usbek, that we never judge things except by a secret return we make upon ourselves. I am not surprised that Black people paint the devil in dazzling whiteness and their gods black as coal; that the Venus of certain peoples has breasts hanging to her thighs; and that, finally, all idolaters have represented their gods with a human face and made them share all their inclinations. It has been very well said that, if triangles made themselves a god, they would give him three sides.

My dear Usbek, when I see men crawling on an atom — that is to say the earth, which is only a point in the universe — proposing themselves directly as models for Providence, I do not know how to reconcile so much extravagance with so much smallness.

*From Paris, the 14th day of the moon of Saphar, 1714.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the comic salon prelude and the philosophical conclusion that human beings imagine divinity in their own image. The turn from social anecdote to anthropological reflection remains clear.

### Main differences
- **Crushed by gout**
  - French: `accablé de goutte`
  - Fresh translation keeps the bodily weight of the phrase.
  - Davidson's `crippled with gout` is effective but somewhat more fixed.
- **Secret return upon ourselves**
  - French: `un retour secret que nous faisons sur nous-mêmes`
  - Fresh translation keeps the reflexive structure.
  - Davidson: `a secret application to ourselves` is elegant but more abstract.
- **Triangles make themselves a god**
  - Fresh translation stays literal.
  - Davidson does too, and this is one of his strongest lines here.
- **Extravagance and smallness**
  - French contrasts `tant d'extravagance` with `tant de petitesse`.
  - Davidson's `littleness` is good, but the fresh version keeps the starker pair.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: comic social observation giving way to philosophical irony.
- **Fresh translation**: tries to keep the swift, almost effortless transition between the two.
- **Davidson**: faithful and readable, with only light abstraction in the reflective prose.
