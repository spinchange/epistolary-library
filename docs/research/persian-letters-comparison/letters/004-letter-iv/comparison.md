## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter IV**

**Zephis to Usbek, at Erzurum**

### Translation
At last that black monster has resolved to drive me to despair. He insists on taking from me my slave Zelide — Zelide, who serves me with such affection, and whose skillful hands carry adornment and grace everywhere. It is not enough for him that this separation should be painful; he also wants it to be dishonorable. The traitor wants to treat as criminal the very grounds of my trust; and because he grows bored behind the door, where I always send him back, he dares suppose that he has heard or seen things I cannot even imagine.

How unhappy I am! Neither my retirement nor my virtue can shelter me from his extravagant suspicions. A vile slave comes to attack me even in your heart, and I must defend myself there! No: I respect myself too much to descend to self-justification. I want no guarantee for my conduct but you yourself, your love, mine, and, if I must say it, dear Usbek, my tears.

*From the seraglio of Fatmé, the 29th day of the moon of Maharram, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson keeps the letter's basic complaint intact: Zephis protests the removal of her attendant, denounces the eunuch's suspicions, and refuses to abase herself by pleading too much. His version is fluent and forceful, but it tends to heighten the rhetoric.

### Main differences
- **Grace-bearing hands**
  - French: `les adroites mains portent partout les ornements et les grâces`
  - Fresh translation: `whose skillful hands carry adornment and grace everywhere`
  - Davidson: `at whose magical touch new charms appear`
  - Davidson turns a concrete image of dressing and embellishment into a more decorative flourish.

- **Self-respect vs pride**
  - French: `j'ai trop de respect pour moi-même`
  - Fresh translation: `I respect myself too much`
  - Davidson: `I am too proud`
  - `Pride` is plausible, but the French stresses dignity rather than vanity.

- **Attack in Usbek's heart**
  - French: `vient m'attaquer jusque dans ton coeur`
  - Fresh translation keeps the image of an intrusion into Usbek's heart.
  - Davidson's `drive me from your heart` is strong, but it shifts from attack to displacement.

- **Range of suspicion**
  - French: `des choses, que je ne sais pas même imaginer`
  - Fresh translation: `things I cannot even imagine`
  - Davidson: `things which my fancy cannot even conceive`
  - Davidson is close, though a little more literary than the French's plain indignation.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: indignant, proud, and compressed; Zephis presents herself as wronged and refuses to grovel.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the clipped emotional pressure and the strong note of self-respect.
- **Davidson**: readable and vivid, but slightly more ornamental in phrasing.

## Working conclusion
Letter IV is substantially faithful in Davidson, but he slightly embellishes Zephis's rhetoric and nudges her self-command toward a more theatrical register.
