## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXIX**

**Usbek to the same**

### Translation
We have nothing more to say about Asia and Europe; let us pass to Africa. One can speak only of its coasts, since its interior is unknown.

The coasts of Barbary, where the Mohammedan religion is established, are no longer as populated as they were in the time of the Romans, for the reasons we have already given. As for the coasts of Guinea, they must have been violently stripped of people ever since, for two hundred years, the petty kings or village chiefs have sold their subjects to the princes of Europe to carry them to their colonies in America.

What is singular is that this America, which receives so many new inhabitants every year, is itself deserted and does not profit from Africa's continual losses. Those slaves, transported into another climate, perish there by the thousand; and the labor of the mines, where both the natives of the country and foreigners are ceaselessly employed, the malignant vapors that come from them, and the mercury that must constantly be used there destroy them without remedy.

Nothing is so extravagant as making innumerable men perish in order to draw from the depths of the earth gold and silver, metals absolutely useless in themselves, and which are riches only because they have been chosen as their signs.

*Paris, the last day of the moon of Chahban, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson gives a compact and generally faithful version of this letter's argument about Africa, slavery, and the demographic waste of colonial mining. He preserves the sequence of claims and the final moral irony about gold and silver, though his English slightly moderates some of the sharper force of the French.

### Main differences
- **Violently emptied coast**
  - French: `furieusement dégarnies`
  - Fresh translation: `violently stripped of people`
  - Davidson: `terribly depopulated`
  - Davidson is accurate in sense, but the French is more vivid and forceful.

- **America as desert**
  - Fresh translation keeps the blunt phrase `is itself deserted`.
  - Davidson's `is itself a desert` is close, though a little more figurative in sound.

- **Mining mortality**
  - French strings together mine labor, poisonous exhalations, and mercury in a dense causal chain.
  - Davidson keeps the list clearly, but the fresh translation stays closer to the compressed accumulation of the original.

- **Gold and silver as signs**
  - French: `ne sont des richesses que parce qu'on les a choisis pour en être les signes`
  - Fresh translation: `are riches only because they have been chosen as their signs`
  - Davidson: `constitute wealth only because they have been chosen as the symbols of it`
  - `Symbols` is elegant, but `signs` is more literal and slightly colder.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: compressed, accusatory, and morally severe.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the density of the argument and the brutality of the demographic claim.
- **Davidson**: quite serviceable, but a little more polished than the French's hard brevity.

## Working conclusion
Letter CXIX is short but forceful. Davidson preserves most of it well, but the fresh translation should retain the violence of terms like `furieusement dégarnies` and the stark reduction of precious metals to arbitrary signs purchased at immense human cost.
