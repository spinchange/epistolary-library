## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXI**

**Rica to [***]**

### Translation
The role of a pretty woman is far graver than people think. Nothing is more serious than what happens in the morning at her toilette, surrounded by her servants; a general of an army does not devote more attention to placing his right wing or his reserve corps than she gives to positioning a beauty patch that may miss its mark, but from which she hopes or foresees success.

What constraint of mind, what constant attention, to reconcile without ceasing the interests of two rivals, to appear neutral to both while yielding herself to each, and to make herself the mediator in every complaint she gives them!

What business it is to keep pleasure parties following one upon another, making them succeed and be reborn without cease, and forestalling every accident that might break them up!

And with all that, the greatest trouble is not to amuse oneself, but to seem to be amused: bore them as much as you like, they will forgive you, provided people can believe they enjoyed themselves thoroughly.

A few days ago I was at a supper given by some women in the country. On the way they kept saying without cease: `At the very least, we must laugh hard and amuse ourselves well.`

We found ourselves rather badly matched, and consequently rather serious. `I must admit,` said one of these women, `that we are amusing ourselves splendidly; there is not in Paris today a party as gay as ours.` As boredom was overtaking me, a woman shook me and said: `Well then, are we not in high spirits?` `Yes,` I answered with a yawn, `I think I shall burst from laughing so much.` Sadness, however, kept triumphing over our efforts; and as for me, I felt myself led from yawn to yawn into a lethargic sleep that put an end to all my pleasures.

*From Paris, the 11th day of the moon of Maharram, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson conveys the basic comic point clearly: fashionable sociability is laborious theater, and the greatest effort goes not into actual pleasure but into performing the appearance of gaiety.

### Main differences
- **`jolie femme`**
  - Fresh translation keeps the simpler `pretty woman`.
  - Davidson's `fine lady` is plausible, but moves the line slightly toward social type and away from physical charm.

- **`mouche` as beauty patch**
  - French: `poster une mouche`
  - Fresh translation makes explicit that this is a beauty patch strategically placed at the toilette.
  - Davidson's `patch` is accurate, but less explanatory for a modern reader.

- **Yielding to both rivals**
  - French: `pendant qu'elle est livrée à l'un et à l'autre`
  - Fresh translation preserves the stronger implication that she is given over to both.
  - Davidson softens this somewhat.

- **Pleasure parties in sequence**
  - French: `faire venir parties de plaisir sur parties`
  - Fresh translation keeps the repetitive sense of pleasure parties piled one upon another.
  - Davidson condenses the phrase.

- **Final movement into boredom**
  - French: `la tristesse triomphoit toujours`
  - Fresh translation keeps sadness as the force that keeps winning.
  - Davidson's `Melancholy ... invaded all our thoughts` is elegant, but less adversarial.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: social, theatrical, and very dry in its mockery of leisure.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the comic military analogies and the weariness beneath the chatter.
- **Davidson**: lively and readable, though slightly smoother in the letter's crueler ironies.

### OCR / cleanup notes
- The English clean text carries a footnote intrusion from the previous letter: `1 Philip III., King of Spain.` interrupts the paragraph about the two rivals.
- Aside from that contamination, the text is largely recoverable.

## Working conclusion
Davidson captures the broad joke of Letter CXI, but the fresh translation keeps more of Montesquieu's exact staging: feminine sociability as campaign planning, diplomacy, and finally compulsory boredom.
