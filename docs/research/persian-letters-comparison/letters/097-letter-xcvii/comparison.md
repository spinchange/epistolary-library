## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XCVII**

**The chief eunuch to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
A number of yellow women from the kingdom of Visapour have arrived here. I bought one for your brother, the governor of Mazenderan, who sent me his sublime command and a hundred tomans a month ago.

I know women well, all the better because they no longer surprise me, and because in me the eyes are not disturbed by the movements of the heart.

I have never seen beauty so regular and so perfect. Her brilliant eyes put life into her face and heighten the radiance of a complexion that could eclipse all the charms of Circassia.

The chief eunuch of a merchant in Ispahan was bargaining with me for her. But she drew herself disdainfully away from his looks, and seemed to seek out mine, as if she wished to tell me that a base merchant was not worthy of her, and that she was destined for a more illustrious husband.

I confess to you that I feel within myself a secret joy when I think of the charms of this beautiful person. I imagine I see her entering your brother's seraglio. I delight in foreseeing the astonishment of all his wives; the imperious grief of some, the silent but more painful affliction of others, the malicious consolation of those who hope for nothing any longer, and the irritated ambition of those who still hope.

I go from one end of the kingdom to the other changing the whole face of a seraglio. What passions I shall stir up! What fears and griefs I am preparing!

Yet, amid this inner turmoil, the outward order will be no less tranquil. Great revolutions will be hidden in the depths of the heart. Sorrows will be swallowed down, joys restrained, obedience no less exact, rules no less inflexible. Sweetness, always constrained to appear, will rise from the very depths of despair.

We observe that the more women we have before our eyes, the less trouble they give us. A greater need to please, less ease in joining together, more examples of submission - all that forges their chains. Some are constantly attentive to the conduct of the others. It seems that in concert with us they work to make themselves more dependent. They do almost half our office and open our eyes when we close them. What am I saying? They are forever stirring the master against their rivals, and do not see how close they stand to those who are punished.

But all that, magnificent lord, is nothing without the master's presence. What can we do with this vain phantom of an authority that is never fully communicated? We represent only feebly one half of yourself. We can show them only a hateful severity. You temper fear with hope; you are more absolute in caressing than in threatening.

Return then, magnificent lord; return to these places and everywhere bear the marks of your empire. Come soften desperate passions; come remove every pretext for failing; come appease love as it murmurs and make duty itself lovable; come at last relieve your faithful eunuchs of a burden that grows heavier every day.

*From the seraglio at Ispahan, the 8th day of the moon of Zilhagé, 1716.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the eunuch's mixture of surveillance, professional pride, aesthetic judgment, and dependence on the absent master. The broad emotional logic of the seraglio is all there.

### Main differences
- **`jaunes` and source-style naming**
  - French explicitly says `femmes jaunes` from `Visapour`.
  - The fresh draft keeps the racialized descriptor and the source transliterations rather than quietly normalizing them away.
  - Davidson is close on this point.

- **Secret joy vs sacred joy**
  - French: `une joie secrète`
  - Fresh translation: `a secret joy`
  - Davidson: `a sacred joy`
  - This is a real semantic shift. The French describes private satisfaction, not sanctity.

- **Forced sweetness**
  - French: `la douceur, toujours contrainte de paroître`
  - Fresh translation keeps outward sweetness as a compelled performance.
  - Davidson's `amiability` is smoother and milder than the French.

- **Half-presence of the master**
  - French: `Nous ne représentons que foiblement la moitié de toi-même`
  - Fresh translation keeps `one half of yourself`.
  - Davidson is idiomatic, but the French's strange formulation is part of the eunuch's logic of delegated authority.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: cold, theatrical, and psychologically acute.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps both the administrative and emotional violence in view.
- **Davidson**: often strong, but occasionally too soft or too grandiose.

### Notable OCR / edition notes
- The English clean text is readable and fairly stable.
- The main issue here is not OCR damage but interpretive drift in a few key words.

## Working conclusion
Davidson handles the scene well overall, but the mistranslation of `joie secrète` and some softer diction reduce the eerie self-consciousness of the chief eunuch's voice.
