## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XXXV**

**Usbek to Gemchid, his cousin, dervish of the brilliant monastery of Tauris**

### Translation
What do you think of the Christians, sublime dervish? Do you believe that on the day of judgment they will be like the unbelieving Turks, who will serve as asses for the Jews and be driven by them at a full gallop into hell? I know very well that they will not go into the dwelling place of the prophets, and that the great Ali did not come for them. But because they have not been fortunate enough to find mosques in their country, do you think they are condemned to eternal punishments, and that God punishes them for not practicing a religion he did not make known to them? I can tell you this: I have often examined these Christians; I questioned them to see whether they had any idea of the great Ali, who was the most beautiful of all men; and I found that they had never heard of him.

They are not like those unbelievers whom our holy prophets put to the sword because they refused to believe in the miracles of heaven; they are rather like those unfortunates who lived in the darkness of idolatry before the divine light came to illuminate the face of our great prophet.

Besides, if one examines their religion closely, one finds in it something like a seed of our doctrines. I have often admired the secrets of Providence, which seems to have wished by that means to prepare them for a general conversion. I have heard of a book by one of their learned men, entitled *Polygamy Triumphant*, in which it is proved that polygamy is commanded for Christians. Their baptism is the image of our legal ablutions; and Christians err only in the efficacy they attribute to that first ablution, which they believe should suffice for all the others. Their priests and monks pray, like us, seven times a day. They hope to enjoy a paradise where, by means of the resurrection of the body, they will taste a thousand delights. They have, like us, appointed fasts, and mortifications by which they hope to bend divine mercy. They worship the good angels and mistrust the evil ones. They have a holy credulity toward the miracles God works through the ministry of his servants. They recognize, like us, the insufficiency of their merits, and their need for an intercessor with God. I see Mohammedanism everywhere, although I do not find Mohammed there. Do what one may, truth escapes and always pierces the darkness that surrounds it. A day will come when the Eternal will see on earth only true believers. Time, which consumes everything, will destroy even errors themselves. All men will be astonished to find themselves under the same standard: everything, even the law itself, will be fulfilled; the divine exemplars will be taken up from the earth and carried into the heavenly archives.

*From Paris, the 20th day of the moon of Zilhagé, 1713.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson conveys the structure and the joke of the letter well: it is a mock-serious account of Christianity as an incomplete anticipation of Islam. His English is generally faithful, but it often becomes slightly more solemn and pious in tone than the French satire requires.

### Main differences
- **The opening grotesque image**
  - French: `serviront d'ânes aux Juifs`
  - Fresh translation: `will serve as asses for the Jews`
  - Davidson: `are to serve the Jews for asses`
  - Davidson is close here; the main point is that the French is intentionally absurd and should stay blunt.

- **The luck of geography**
  - French: `ils n'ont pas été assez heureux pour trouver des mosquées dans leur pays`
  - Fresh translation: `they have not been fortunate enough to find mosques in their country`
  - Davidson: `they have not been fortunate enough to find mosques in their country`
  - This is one of Davidson's closest and strongest renderings in the letter.

- **`Semence` of doctrine**
  - French: `comme une semence de nos dogmes`
  - Fresh translation: `something like a seed of our doctrines`
  - Davidson: `some rudiments of our doctrines`
  - `Rudiments` is sensible, but the image of a `seed` better preserves the providential-development metaphor.

- **The line about Mohammedanism everywhere**
  - French: `Je vois partout le mahométisme, quoique je n'y trouve point Mahomet`
  - Fresh translation: `I see Mohammedanism everywhere, although I do not find Mohammed there`
  - Davidson: `Throughout their religion I find traces of Mohammedanism, although there is no word of Mohammed`
  - Davidson makes the thought slightly more explanatory and less epigrammatic.

- **Truth breaking through darkness**
  - French: `la vérité s'échappe, et perce toujours les ténèbres`
  - Fresh translation keeps the paired verbs `escapes` and `pierces`.
  - Davidson: `truth will break through the darkest clouds`
  - Davidson produces an elegant proverb-like sentence, but it is a freer recasting.

- **The close of providential history**
  - French: `tout, jusqu'à la loi, sera consommé`
  - Fresh translation: `everything, even the law itself, will be fulfilled`
  - Davidson: `everything, the law itself, will be accomplished`
  - Davidson is close, though the slightly scriptural resonance of `fulfilled` suits the French well here.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: mock-devout, triumphalist, and knowingly absurd; the comedy depends on complete seriousness of expression.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the theological straight face while preserving the letter's satirical overconfidence.
- **Davidson**: usually accurate, but a bit more reverent and less deadpan than the French.

## Working conclusion
Letter XXXV comes across clearly in Davidson, but the fresh translation keeps better hold of the original's satirical poise: Christianity is treated not as an enemy creed but as a providential near-miss, and that joke works best when the language stays exact and unruffled.
