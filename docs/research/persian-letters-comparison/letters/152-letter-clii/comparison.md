## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CLII**

**Narsit to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Roxane and Zélis wished to go into the country; I did not think I ought to refuse them. Happy Usbek! You have faithful wives and vigilant slaves. I command in a place where virtue seems to have chosen a refuge for itself. Be assured that nothing will happen there that your eyes could not bear.

A misfortune has occurred that puts me in great distress. Some Armenian merchants newly arrived in Ispahan had brought one of your letters for me. I sent a slave to fetch it; he was robbed on his way back, so that the letter is lost. Write to me promptly, then, for I imagine that in this turn of affairs you must have matters of consequence to tell me.

*From the seraglio of Fatmé, the 6th day of the moon of Rebiab I, 1719.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson again gives the broad sense accurately, but this letter matters because its deceptive calm depends on small tonal choices. Narsit is reassuring Usbek while quietly reporting the convenient loss of a crucial letter.

### Main differences
- **Virtue's `asile`**
  - French: `la vertu semble s'être choisi un asile`
  - Fresh translation: `virtue seems to have chosen a refuge for itself`
  - Davidson: `virtue seems to have made its home in the abode which I rule`
  - Davidson expands and domesticates the image; the French has the sharper idea of a sanctuary or asylum.

- **What Usbek's eyes can `soutenir`**
  - French: `rien que tes yeux ne puissent soutenir`
  - Fresh translation: `nothing ... that your eyes could not bear`
  - Davidson: `nothing shall happen of which you would not approve`
  - This is an interpretive change. `Soutenir` suggests endurance or toleration, not necessarily moral approval.

- **`Dans ce changement`**
  - Fresh translation: `in this turn of affairs`
  - Davidson: `at this juncture`
  - Davidson is idiomatic, but the French more strongly suggests a change in the state of things.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: outwardly reassuring, inwardly evasive.
- **Fresh translation**: tries to preserve the suspicious polish of Narsit's voice.
- **Davidson**: fluent and accurate in outline, though slightly more straightforward than the French's self-protective phrasing.

## Working conclusion
Letter CLII is short, but it is narratively important:
- Narsit presents the seraglio as secure
- flatters Usbek with talk of faithful wives and vigilant slaves
- and then reports the conveniently lost letter in language that Davidson renders competently, if a bit more directly than the French
