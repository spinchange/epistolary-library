## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter II**

**Usbek to the chief black eunuch, at his seraglio in Ispahan**

### Translation
You are the faithful guardian of the most beautiful women in Persia; I have entrusted to you what I hold most dear in the world: in your hands are the keys to those fatal doors that open only for me. While you keep watch over this precious deposit of my heart, it rests and enjoys complete security. You stand guard in the silence of night as in the tumult of day. Your tireless care sustains virtue when it begins to falter. If the women under your watch wished to stray from their duty, you would rob them even of the hope of doing so. You are the scourge of vice and the pillar of fidelity.

You command them, and you obey them. You carry out all their wishes blindly, and you make them in turn obey the laws of the seraglio; you take pride in rendering them the lowliest services; you submit with respect and fear to their lawful orders; you serve them like the slave of their slaves. But by a return of authority, you command like a master, as I myself do, whenever you fear any slackening in the laws of modesty and decency.

Always remember the nothingness from which I raised you, when you were the least of my slaves, to place you where you are now and entrust to you the delights of my heart: keep yourself in profound humility before those who share my love; but at the same time make them feel how utterly dependent they are. Procure for them every pleasure that can remain innocent; divert their anxieties; amuse them with music, dancing, and delicious drinks; persuade them to gather together often. If they wish to go into the country, you may take them there; but have every man struck down who presents himself before them. Exhort them to cleanliness, which is the image of the soul's purity; speak to them of me from time to time. I should like to see them again in that charming place which they adorn. Farewell.

*From Tabriz, the 18th day of the moon of Saphar, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson is again broadly faithful in structure and argument. He preserves the paradoxical authority of the eunuch's role — servant and master at once — and he conveys the mixture of tenderness, domination, and surveillance that defines Usbek's voice in this letter.

### Main differences
- **Guardian / keeper / deposit imagery**
  - French: `gardien fidèle`, `dépôt précieux de mon coeur`
  - Fresh translation keeps the language of guardianship and deposit.
  - Davidson shifts slightly toward emotional paraphrase with `keeper` and `storehouse of my affections`.
  - `storehouse of my affections` is vivid, but more interpretive than the French.

- **Hope of transgression**
  - French: `tu leur en ferois perdre l'espérance`
  - Fresh translation: `you would rob them even of the hope of doing so`
  - Davidson: `you would destroy their hopes in the bud`
  - Davidson sharpens the idiom, but the French is colder and more juridical.

- **Column / pillar of fidelity**
  - French: `la colonne de la fidélité`
  - Fresh translation: `the pillar of fidelity`
  - Davidson: `the very monument of fidelity`
  - `monument` is stronger and more ornamental; `pillar` stays closer to the image in the French.

- **Return of command**
  - French: `Mais, par un retour d'empire`
  - Fresh translation: `But by a return of authority`
  - Davidson: `But, resuming your power`
  - Davidson smooths the phrase into idiomatic English, but loses some of the abrupt rhetorical turn.

- **Moral vocabulary**
  - French: `pudeur et de la modestie`
  - Fresh translation: `modesty and decency`
  - Davidson: `chastity and modesty`
  - Davidson moralizes the line more strongly by introducing `chastity`.

- **Status reversal and humiliation**
  - French: `Souviens-toi toujours du néant d'où je t'ai fait sortir`
  - Fresh translation: `Always remember the nothingness from which I raised you`
  - Davidson: `Never forget that I raised you from the lowest position`
  - Davidson is clear, but the French has a more absolute, humiliating force in `néant`.

- **Violence at the country outing**
  - French: `fais faire main-basse sur tous les hommes`
  - Fresh translation: `have every man struck down`
  - Davidson: `lay hands on every man`
  - Davidson softens the violence somewhat. The French phrase is harsher and more threatening.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: imperious, intimate, and chillingly administrative. Love, domination, and discipline are fused together.
- **Fresh translation**: tries to keep the cold precision of Usbek's authority while remaining readable in modern English.
- **Davidson**: elegant and readable, but often slightly softens the brutality or converts stark formulations into polished literary phrasing.

## Working conclusion
Letter II confirms a pattern already visible in Letter I:
- Davidson is usually faithful at the level of broad meaning
- but he often prefers smoother, more literary English over the original's harder edges
- and in this letter especially, that means slightly softening the language of domination, humiliation, and coercion.
