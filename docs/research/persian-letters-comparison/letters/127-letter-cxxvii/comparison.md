## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXXVII**

**Rica to Usbek, at \*\*\*.**

### Translation
I am expecting you here tomorrow; meanwhile I am sending you your letters from Isfahan. Mine report that the ambassador of the Great Mogul has received orders to leave the kingdom. It is added that the prince, the king's uncle, who is charged with his education, has been arrested, taken to a castle where he is very closely guarded, and stripped of all his honors. I am touched by this prince's fate, and I pity him.

I confess it to you, Usbek: I have never seen anyone's tears flow without being moved by them. I feel humanity for the unfortunate, as though they alone were truly human; and even the great, toward whom I find hardness in my heart while they are exalted, I love as soon as they fall.

Indeed, what have they to do with a useless tenderness in prosperity? It comes too close to equality. They greatly prefer respect, which asks for no return. But as soon as they have fallen from greatness, only our lament can recall the idea of it to them.

I find something very naive, and even very great, in the words of a prince who, on the verge of falling into the hands of his enemies, seeing his courtiers around him in tears, said to them: 'I feel by your tears that I am still your king.'

*Paris, the 3rd day of the moon of Chalval, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson handles this short letter well. He preserves Rica's quick movement from court news to a meditation on pity, hierarchy, and the strange dignity of fallen greatness.

### Main differences
- **`comme s'il n'y avoit qu'eux qui fussent hommes`**
  - Fresh translation keeps the stronger formulation: the unfortunate seem to be the only true humans.
  - Davidson softens slightly with `as if they only were human`, which is close but a little smoother.

- **`nos plaintes`**
  - Fresh translation uses `our lament` to keep the public and elegiac coloring.
  - Davidson's `our lamentation` is accurate, though a touch heavier in English.

- **`naïf` joined to `grand`**
  - The French admires the prince's words as both simple and elevated.
  - Davidson's `admirable simplicity` is good, but the fresh draft keeps the tension between unaffected candor and grandeur a little more plainly.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: compact, humane, and reflective.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the letter's sympathy without losing its sense of rank and ceremony.
- **Davidson**: very close overall, with only minor smoothing.
