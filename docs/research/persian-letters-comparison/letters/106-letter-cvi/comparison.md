## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CVI**

**Rhedi to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
In one of your letters you spoke to me a great deal about the sciences and arts cultivated in the West. You are going to think me a barbarian; but I do not know whether the usefulness one derives from them compensates men for the bad use made of them every day.

I have heard it said that the invention of bombs alone has taken freedom away from all the peoples of Europe. Princes, no longer able to entrust the defense of fortified places to townsmen, who would surrender at the first bomb, found there a pretext for maintaining large bodies of regular troops, with which they afterwards oppressed their subjects.

You know that since the invention of gunpowder there are no longer any impregnable places; that is to say, Usbek, that there is no longer any refuge on earth from injustice and violence.

I am always afraid that people will finally discover some secret that offers a shorter way to make men perish, to destroy whole peoples and nations.

You have read the historians; pay close attention to them: almost all monarchies have been founded only on ignorance of the arts, and have been destroyed only because the arts were cultivated too much. The ancient empire of Persia can furnish us with a domestic example of this.

I have not been long in Europe; but I have heard sensible people speak of the ravages of chemistry: it seems to be a fourth scourge, ruining men and destroying them piecemeal but continually, whereas war, plague, and famine destroy them in masses, but only at intervals.

What has the invention of the compass and the discovery of so many peoples done for us except communicate their diseases to us rather than their riches? Gold and silver had been established, by a general convention, as the price of all goods and the pledge of their value, because these metals were rare and useless for any other purpose: what did it matter to us, then, that they became more common, and that, in order to mark the value of a commodity, we should have two or three signs instead of one? It only made things more inconvenient.

But on the other hand this discovery has been very harmful to the countries that were discovered. Entire nations were destroyed, and the men who escaped death were reduced to a slavery so harsh that Muslims shudder on hearing the account of it.

Happy the ignorance of the children of Mohammed! Amiable simplicity, so dear to our holy Prophet, you always call back to me the innocence of ancient times and the tranquility that reigned in the hearts of our first fathers.

*From Venice, the 2nd day of the moon of Rhamazan, 1717.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson accurately conveys the letter's general anti-modern complaint: military technology destroys liberty, technical knowledge expands the means of killing, commerce spreads disease, and simplicity is nostalgically opposed to European refinement.

### Main differences
- **Compensation for misuse**
  - French: `je ne sais si l'utilité ... dédommage les hommes du mauvais usage`
  - Fresh translation keeps the sense of usefulness failing to compensate for habitual abuse.
  - Davidson is close, but a little more generalized.

- **Bombs and political liberty**
  - French says the invention of bombs `ôt[e] la liberté à tous les peuples d'Europe`
  - Fresh translation keeps the sweeping claim that bombs took freedom away.
  - Davidson preserves the sense well.

- **`chimie` vs `alchemy`**
  - French: `les ravages de la chimie`
  - Fresh translation keeps the straightforward `chemistry`.
  - Davidson's `alchemy` is a real interpretive shift.

- **Disease rather than wealth**
  - French: `nous communiquer leurs maladies plutôt que leurs richesses`
  - Fresh translation preserves the active idea of foreign peoples communicating diseases to Europe rather than riches.
  - Davidson's `given us more diseases than wealth` is readable, but a bit looser.

- **Date discrepancy**
  - French date: `le 2 de la lune de Rhamazan, 1717`
  - Davidson: `the 5th of the moon of Rhamazan, 1717`
  - This appears to be a substantive discrepancy rather than a stylistic difference.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: sweeping, suspicious of innovation, and rhetorically aphoristic.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the sequence of accusations plain and hard-edged.
- **Davidson**: generally faithful, but sometimes a little more explanatory and less exact in key terms.

### OCR / cleanup notes
- The English clean text is broadly usable for comparison.
- No major OCR break obscures the argument, though the date should be checked against the scan because of the `2nd` / `5th` discrepancy.

## Working conclusion
Davidson carries the argument of Letter CVI well, but a fresh translation matters in the sharper anti-technical vocabulary - especially `chemistry`, the rhetoric of bombs and liberty, and the stark claim that overseas discovery spread disease more than wealth.
