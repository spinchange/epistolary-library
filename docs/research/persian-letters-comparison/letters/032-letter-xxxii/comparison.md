## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XXXII**

**Rica to ∗∗∗.**

### Translation
The other day I went to see a house where about three hundred people are maintained rather poorly. I was soon done, for neither the church nor the buildings deserve to be looked at. Those in that house were cheerful enough; several of them were playing cards, or other games I do not know. As I was going out, one of those men was also going out; and, having heard me ask the way to the Marais, which is the quarter farthest from Paris, he said to me, `I am going there, and I will take you there; follow me.` He guided me wonderfully, got me through every difficulty, and skillfully saved me from carriages and coaches. We were almost there when curiosity took hold of me. `My good friend,` I said to him, `might I know who you are?` `I am blind, sir,` he answered. `What!` I said to him, `you are blind! And why did you not ask that honest man who was playing cards with you to guide us?` `He is blind too,` he answered. `For four hundred years there have been three hundred blind men in that house where you found me. But I must leave you. There is the street you were asking for. I am going to put myself into the crowd; I am entering that church, where, I swear to you, I shall hinder people more than they will hinder me.`

*From Paris, the 17th day of the moon of Chalval, 1712.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson renders this brief anecdotal letter cleanly and preserves its deadpan surprise. The blind guide's competence and Rica's belated realization come through well. The differences are minor and mostly concern idiom.

### Main differences
- **Poorly maintained**
  - French: `où l'on entretient environ trois cents personnes assez pauvrement`
  - Fresh translation: `where about three hundred people are maintained rather poorly`
  - Davidson: `where a meagre provision is made for some three hundred people`
  - Davidson is close, though a little more decorative in phrasing.

- **Deserve to be looked at**
  - French: `ne méritent pas d'être regardés`
  - Fresh translation keeps the straightforward visual wording.
  - Davidson's `do not deserve much attention` is idiomatic, but slightly less concrete.

- **Saved me from carriages and coaches**
  - French: `me sauva adroitement des carrosses et des voitures`
  - Fresh translation preserves the physical comedy.
  - Davidson is close and effective here.

- **I am going to put myself into the crowd**
  - French: `je vais me mettre dans la foule`
  - Fresh translation keeps the literal movement.
  - Davidson smooths this into `I am going with the crowd`, which is less vivid.

- **I shall hinder people more than they will hinder me**
  - French: `j'embarrasserai plus les gens qu'ils ne m'embarrasseront`
  - Fresh translation preserves the reversal.
  - Davidson's `people will be less in my way than I will be in theirs` is an accurate and graceful equivalent.
