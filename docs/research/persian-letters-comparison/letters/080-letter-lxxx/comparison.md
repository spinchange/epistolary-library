## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXXX**

**The chief eunuch to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Yesterday some Armenians brought to the seraglio a young Circassian slave whom they wished to sell. I had her brought into the secret apartments, undressed her, and examined her with a judge's eyes; and the more I examined her, the more graces I found in her. A virginal modesty seemed to want to hide them from my sight; I saw all it cost her to obey: she blushed to see herself naked, even before me, who, exempt from the passions that can alarm modesty, am lifeless under the empire of that sex, and who, as the minister of modesty in its freest acts, bear only chaste looks and can inspire only innocence.

As soon as I judged her worthy of you, I lowered my eyes; I threw a scarlet cloak over her, put a gold ring on her finger; I prostrated myself at her feet, I adored her as the queen of your heart; I paid the Armenians, and stole her from every eye. Happy Usbek! You possess more beauties than all the palaces of the East contain. What pleasure for you, on your return, to find all that Persia has most ravishing, and to see graces reborn in your seraglio in proportion as time and possession work to destroy them!

*From the seraglio of Fatme, the 1st day of the moon of Rebiab I, 1715.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson conveys the main scene clearly: the eunuch inspects a newly purchased Circassian slave, certifies her beauty for Usbek, and frames his own role as paradoxically chaste within an erotic economy of possession. The English is effective, but a few key phrases shift the force of the eunuch's self-description.

### Main differences
- **Title expansion**
  - French title: `LE GRAND EUNUQUE A USBEK.`
  - Fresh translation: `The chief eunuch to Usbek`
  - Davidson: `The chief black Eunuch to Usbek`
  - `Black` is not present in the French title of this letter, even if it may be inferable from the wider seraglio context.

- **`pudeur virginale`**
  - Fresh translation: `a virginal modesty`
  - Davidson: `a virginal shame`
  - `Shame` is possible, but `pudeur` is closer to modesty or bashfulness than to shame.

- **The eunuch's relation to sex**
  - French: `je suis inanimé sous l'empire de ce sexe`
  - Fresh translation: `I am lifeless under the empire of that sex`
  - Davidson: `entirely delivered from the dominion of the sex`
  - Davidson reverses the nuance. The French stresses impotence and inertness, not liberation.

- **`je la dérobai à tous les yeux`**
  - Fresh translation: `I stole her from every eye`
  - Davidson: `I hid her from every eye`
  - Davidson is accurate in sense, but `dérobai` keeps a sharper sense of removal and appropriation.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: ceremonious, proprietary, and chillingly self-justifying.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the blend of judicial inspection, ritual devotion, and possessive triumph.
- **Davidson**: lucid and often close, but slightly more smoothing in the eunuch's self-exculpating rhetoric.

### OCR / cleanup notes
- The English clean text contains visible OCR corruption in `rjiy eyes` and `her-worthy`.
- The final sentence also ends with a dangling `1`, likely a note marker left in the text.

## Working conclusion
Letter LXXX is short but revealing: Davidson preserves the episode, yet the fresh translation better keeps the strange and important paradox that the eunuch is not free of the sexual order but inert within it.
