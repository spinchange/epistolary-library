## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXXXII**

**Nargum, Persian envoy in Muscovy, to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Of all the nations in the world, my dear Usbek, there is none that has surpassed the Tartars either in glory or in the greatness of their conquests. This people is the true master of the universe; all the others seem made to serve it: it is at once the founder and the destroyer of empires; at all times it has given on earth proofs of its power; in every age it has been the scourge of nations.

The Tartars conquered China twice, and they still keep it under their obedience.

They rule the vast lands that form the empire of the Mogul.

Masters of Persia, they sit on the throne of Cyrus and Gustasp. They have subdued Muscovy. Under the name of Turks, they have made immense conquests in Europe, Asia, and Africa; and they dominate these three parts of the universe.

And, to speak of more distant times, it is from them that almost all the peoples who overthrew the Roman empire came.

What are Alexander's conquests in comparison with those of Genghis Khan?

All this victorious nation has lacked is historians to celebrate the memory of its marvels.

How many immortal deeds have been buried in oblivion! How many empires founded by them, whose origin we do not know! That warlike nation, occupied solely with its present glory, certain of conquering in every age, gave no thought to making itself famous in the future by the memory of its past conquests.

*Moscow, the 4th day of the moon of Rebiab I, 1715.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson presents the letter's panegyric to the Tartars clearly and forcefully. He keeps the catalogue of conquests and the broad rhetorical arc from present dominion to historical oblivion. The main differences are small but meaningful shifts in scale, emphasis, and exactness.

### Main differences
- **Glory and greatness vs splendour and magnitude**
  - French: `en gloire, ni dans la grandeur des conquêtes`
  - Fresh translation: `in glory or in the greatness of their conquests`
  - Davidson: `in the splendour and magnitude of its conquests`
  - Davidson is stately, but the French is a little simpler and more martial.

- **True master of the universe**
  - Fresh translation: `the true master of the universe`
  - Davidson: `the veritable ruler of the earth`
  - Davidson slightly narrows the scale from `univers` to `earth`.

- **Who destroyed Rome?**
  - French: `presque tous les peuples qui ont renversé l'empire romain`
  - Fresh translation: `almost all the peoples who overthrew the Roman empire`
  - Davidson: `some of those races who overthrew the Roman empire`
  - This is a notable reduction in scope.

- **Memory of marvels**
  - French: `la mémoire de ses merveilles`
  - Fresh translation: `the memory of its marvels`
  - Davidson: `its achievements`
  - `Achievements` is serviceable, but `merveilles` is more grandiose and legend-like.

- **Closing emphasis on present glory**
  - French: `uniquement occupée de sa gloire présente`
  - Fresh translation: `occupied solely with its present glory`
  - Davidson: `occupied exclusively with its immediate glory`
  - Davidson is close, though `immediate` has a slightly different temporal flavor.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: grand, declamatory, and historical in sweep.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the high rhetorical temperature without introducing unnecessary ornament.
- **Davidson**: very readable and often impressive, but slightly more polished and expansive in register.

## Working conclusion
Letter LXXXII is close overall, but Davidson repeatedly scales the rhetoric a little differently - smoothing `glory`, narrowing `universe` to `earth`, and reducing `almost all` to `some` - which makes the fresh translation useful as a corrective.
