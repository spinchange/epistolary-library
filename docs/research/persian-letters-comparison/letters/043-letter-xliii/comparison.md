## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XLIII**

**Usbek to Pharan, at the gardens of Fatmé**

### Translation
Receive joy into your heart, and recognize these sacred characters: have the chief eunuch and the steward of my gardens kiss them. I forbid them to lay a hand on you until my return; tell them to buy the missing eunuch. Fulfill your duty as if you always had me before your eyes; for know that the greater my kindnesses are, the more you will be punished if you abuse them.

*From Paris, the 25th day of the moon of Rhégeb, 1713.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson is very close on this short letter. The main differences are tonal and lexical: the French is briskly authoritarian, and a fresh translation can keep its direct imperatives and compact threat with slightly less smoothing.

### Main differences
- **`Sacrés caractères`**
  - French: `reconnaissez ces sacrés caractères`
  - Fresh translation: `recognize these sacred characters`
  - Davidson: `acknowledge these sacred characters`
  - Both are workable, though `recognize` keeps the more immediate force of the French verb.

- **Kissing `them`**
  - French: `faites-les baiser`
  - Fresh translation: `have ... kiss them`
  - Davidson: `Let ... kiss this letter`
  - Davidson makes explicit what is only implied in the French.

- **The physical prohibition**
  - French: `Je leur défends de mettre la main sur vous`
  - Fresh translation: `I forbid them to lay a hand on you`
  - Davidson: `I prohibit them from attempting anything against you`
  - Davidson generalizes a vivid and specifically physical phrase.

- **The final warning**
  - French: `plus mes bontés sont grandes plus vous serez puni si vous en abusez`
  - Fresh translation: `the greater my kindnesses are, the more you will be punished if you abuse them`
  - Davidson: `as my bounty is great, so, if you abuse it, will the measure of your punishment be`
  - Davidson is elegant, but the French is more direct and menacing.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: brief, decisive, and unmistakably authoritarian.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to preserve the rapid movement from favor to threat.
- **Davidson**: very close in substance, though slightly more formalized in cadence.

## Working conclusion
Letter XLIII confirms that Davidson can be very close when the letter is brief and structurally simple. Even here, though, a fresh translation better keeps the clipped authority of Usbek's commands and the blunt force of his conditional mercy.
