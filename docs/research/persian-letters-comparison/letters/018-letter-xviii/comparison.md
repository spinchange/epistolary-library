## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XVIII**

**Méhémet Ali, servant of the prophets, to Usbek**

### Translation
You are always asking us questions that have been put a thousand times to our holy Prophet. Why do you not read the traditions of the doctors? Why do you not go to that pure source of all intelligence? You would find all your doubts resolved there.

Unhappy men, forever entangled in the things of earth, who have never fixed a steady eye on those of heaven, and who revere the condition of the mollaks without daring either to embrace it or follow it!

Profane men, who never enter into the secrets of the Eternal, your lights are like the darkness of the abyss, and the reasonings of your mind are like the dust your feet raise when the sun is at its midday height in the burning month of Chahban.

The zenith of your mind does not reach the nadir of the least of the imams. Your vain philosophy is that lightning which announces storm and darkness. You are in the middle of the tempest, and wander at the mercy of the winds.

It is very easy to answer your difficulty. To do so, one need only tell you what happened one day to our holy Prophet when, tempted by Christians and tried by Jews, he equally confounded both.

The Jew Abdias Ibesalon asked him why God had forbidden the eating of swine's flesh. Not without reason, replied the Prophet: it is an unclean animal, and I will convince you of it. He formed upon his hand, with mud, the figure of a man; he threw it to the ground and cried: Arise! Immediately a man rose and said: I am Japheth, son of Noah. Were your hair so white when you died? the holy Prophet asked him. No, he replied; but when you woke me I thought the day of judgment had come, and I was so frightened that my hair turned white at once.

Now then, the envoy of God said to him, tell me the whole history of Noah's ark. Japheth obeyed and recounted exactly all that happened in the first months, after which he spoke thus:

We put the filth of all the animals on one side of the ark, which made it lean so greatly that we were in mortal fear, especially our women, who lamented in fine fashion. Our father Noah, having gone to the council of God, was commanded to take the elephant and turn its head toward the side that leaned. That great animal made so much filth that a pig was born from it. Do you think, Usbek, that since that time we have abstained from it and regarded it as an unclean animal for nothing?

But since the pig stirred up that filth every day, such a stench arose in the ark that he himself could not keep from sneezing, and out of his nose came a rat that went gnawing everything before it. This became so unbearable to Noah that he thought it proper to consult God again. God ordered him to strike the lion a heavy blow on the forehead; the lion sneezed too, and a cat came out of his nose. Do you still think those animals are not unclean? What do you say to that?

So when you do not perceive the reason for the impurity of certain things, it is because you are ignorant of many others, and do not know what has passed between God, the angels, and men. You do not know the history of eternity. You have not read the books written in heaven. What has been revealed to you is only a small part of the divine library; and those who, like us, come nearer to it while still in this life remain nevertheless in obscurity and darkness. Farewell. May Muhammad be in your heart.

*At Qom, the last day of the moon of Chahban, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves both the doctrinal swagger and the absurd sacred anecdote. The letter remains intelligible and often vivid in his version, but he repeatedly smooths or heightens the rhetoric in ways that make the satire feel slightly more reverent than the French.

### Main differences
- **Dust raised by feet**
  - French: `la poussière que vos pieds font élever`
  - Fresh translation: `the dust your feet raise`
  - Davidson: `the dust which rises as you walk`
  - Davidson keeps the image, but with a more polished and less bodily phrasing.

- **Equally confounded both parties**
  - French: `il confondit également et les uns et les autres`
  - Fresh translation: `he equally confounded both`
  - Davidson: `effectually silenced both parties`
  - Davidson shifts from confounding them to merely silencing them.

- **Grotesque physicality of the ark story**
  - French: `ordures`, `puanteur`, `il sortit de son nez un rat`
  - Fresh translation keeps the coarse bluntness of filth, stench, and creatures coming out of noses.
  - Davidson is accurate in outline, but more euphemistic and narratively smoothed.

- **Pig stirring the filth**
  - French: `le cochon remuoit tous les jours ces ordures`
  - Fresh translation: `the pig stirred up that filth every day`
  - Davidson: `the pig wallowed every day among the filth`
  - `wallowed` is vivid, but it changes the precise action described.

- **Divine library**
  - French: `une petite partie de la bibliothèque divine`
  - Fresh translation: `a small part of the divine library`
  - Davidson: `but an insignificant part of the divine library`
  - Davidson adds a stronger evaluative note with `insignificant`.

- **Approaching heaven in this life**
  - French: `ceux qui, comme nous, en approchent de plus près`
  - Fresh translation: `those who, like us, come nearer to it`
  - Davidson: `those who, like us, have approached so near that they may be said to live the heavenly life`
  - Davidson expands the boast into a more explicit devotional claim.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: pompous, comic, authoritarian, and deliberately overblown.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the deadpan delivery that lets the absurdity show through.
- **Davidson**: serviceable and often close, but somewhat more reverent and sonorous than the satire requires.

### Notable OCR / edition notes
- The English clean text is usable, but it still carries note markers and inherited cleanup noise, especially in the opening paragraphs.
- The title line preserves uneven capitalization (`TO UsBEK, AT ERZEROUM.`).
- No major sentence-level loss occurs, but the letter would still benefit from a later manual cleanup pass.

## Working conclusion
Letter XVIII compares well because Davidson keeps the architecture of the joke intact. The main thing he changes is tone: the French is straighter-faced and nastier in its comic theology, while Davidson often makes it sound more dignified than it is.
