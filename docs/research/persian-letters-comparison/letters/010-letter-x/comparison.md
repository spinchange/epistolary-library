## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter X**

**Mirza to his friend Usbek, at Erzurum**

### Translation
You alone could compensate me for Rica's absence, and only Rica could console me for yours. We miss you, Usbek: you were the soul of our company. How much violence it takes to break bonds formed by both heart and mind.

We dispute a great deal here, and our disputes ordinarily turn on morality. Yesterday the question was whether men are made happy by pleasures and satisfactions of the senses, or by the practice of virtue. I have often heard you say that men are born to be virtuous, and that justice is a quality as proper to them as existence itself. Explain to me, I beg you, what you mean.

I have spoken to some mollaks, who drive me to despair with their quotations from the Koran; for I do not speak to them as a true believer, but as a man, as a citizen, as the father of a family. Farewell.

*From Isfahan, the last day of the moon of Saphar, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson is very close here. The letter is brief, epistolary, and philosophical, and he preserves all three qualities with little interference.

### Main differences
- **Soul of the group**
  - French: `tu étois l'âme de notre société`
  - Fresh translation: `you were the soul of our company`
  - Davidson: `the very life of our circle`
  - Davidson's phrase is elegant but slightly more colloquial than the French metaphor.

- **Justice as natural quality**
  - French: `la justice est une qualité qui leur est aussi propre que l'existence`
  - Fresh translation stays close to the claim that justice properly belongs to human beings.
  - Davidson: `justice is as indispensable to existence as life itself`
  - That shifts from an essential quality to a necessity, which is a real conceptual change.

- **Interlocutor stance**
  - French: `comme homme, comme citoyen, comme père de famille`
  - Fresh translation preserves the serial self-definitions.
  - Davidson does too, but his sentence is slightly more formalized.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: friendly, brisk, and philosophically pointed.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the letter's quick movement from affection to moral inquiry.
- **Davidson**: strong and accurate, with only minor conceptual smoothing.
