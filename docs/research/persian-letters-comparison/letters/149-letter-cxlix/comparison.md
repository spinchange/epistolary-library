## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXLIX**

**Narsit to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
The chief eunuch has just died, magnificent lord. As I am the oldest of your slaves, I have taken his place until you make known on whom you wish to cast your eyes.

Two days after his death, one of your letters addressed to him was brought to me. I was careful not to open it; I wrapped it up with respect and locked it away until you make known your sacred will.

Yesterday, in the middle of the night, a slave came to tell me that he had found a young man in the seraglio. I got up, examined the matter, and found that it was a vision.

I kiss your feet, sublime lord, and I beg you to rely on my zeal, my experience, and my old age.

*From the seraglio at Ispahan, the 5th day of the moon of Gemmadi I, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson is quite close in this short letter. He keeps Narsit's posture of dutiful subordination and preserves the combination of procedural caution and deferential self-advertisement.

### Main differences
- **Image of selection**
  - French: `jusques à ce que tu aies fait connoître sur qui tu veux jeter les yeux`
  - Fresh translation: `until you make known on whom you wish to cast your eyes`
  - Davidson: `until you make known whom you select`
  - Davidson gives the sense clearly, but the French has a more courtly visual metaphor.

- **Treatment of the letter**
  - French: `Je me suis bien gardé de l'ouvrir`
  - Fresh translation: `I was careful not to open it`
  - Davidson: `I refrained from opening it`
  - Davidson is accurate; the fresh translation keeps slightly more of the self-protective emphasis.

- **`C'était une vision`**
  - Fresh translation: `it was a vision`
  - Davidson: `it had been a vision`
  - This is a tiny difference, but the French phrasing leaves the false alarm deliberately abrupt and odd.

- **Closing credentials**
  - French: `mon zèle, mon expérience et ma vieillesse`
  - Fresh translation keeps `old age` as part of Narsit's claim to authority.
  - Davidson does the same and is close here.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: cautious, ceremonious, and faintly self-serving.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to preserve the mixture of obedience and opportunism.
- **Davidson**: very close, with only modest smoothing.

## Working conclusion
Letter CXLIX is a close correspondence overall. Its main value in the sequence is narrative rather than translational:
- it marks the death of the chief eunuch
- installs Narsit provisionally
- and keeps the atmosphere of suspicion alive while sounding outwardly calm
