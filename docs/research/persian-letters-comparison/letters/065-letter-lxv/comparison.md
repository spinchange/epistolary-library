## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXV**

**Usbek to his wives, at the seraglio of Isfahan**

### Translation
I learn that the seraglio is in disorder and filled with quarrels and internal divisions. What did I recommend to you when I left, if not peace and good understanding? You promised it to me. Was it in order to deceive me?

It is you who would be deceived if I chose to follow the advice the chief eunuch gives me, if I chose to use my authority to make you live as my exhortations required of you.

I do not know how to use such violent means until I have tried all the others. So, for your own sake, do what you did not wish to do for mine.

The chief eunuch has great reason to complain. He says you show him no regard. How can you reconcile that conduct with the modesty of your condition? Is it not to him that your virtue is entrusted during my absence? It is a sacred treasure, and he is its depositary. But the contempt you show him is a sign that those charged with making you live by the laws of honor are burdensome to you.

Change your conduct then, I beg you, and make it possible for me another time to reject the proposals made to me against your freedom and your repose.

For I would like to make you forget that I am your master, so that I may remember only that I am your husband.

*At Paris, the 5th day of the moon of Chahban, 1714.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson keeps the basic structure of the letter: disorder in the seraglio, warning about the chief eunuch's complaints, and Usbek's self-presentation as a husband who supposedly refrains from harsher authority.

### Main differences
- **Conditional irony**
  - French: `C'est vous qui seriez trompées`
  - Fresh translation: `It is you who would be deceived`
  - Davidson: `It is you who will be deceived`
  - Davidson slightly hardens the warning. The French conditional better preserves Usbek's pose of still holding violence in reserve.

- **`Dépositaire`**
  - Fresh translation: `depositary`
  - Davidson: `guardian`
  - `Guardian` is clear, but the French leans more explicitly toward custodial and quasi-legal language.

- **Laws of honor**
  - French: `les lois de l'honneur`
  - Fresh translation: `the laws of honor`
  - Davidson: `the paths of honour`
  - Davidson smooths the phrase. The fresh translation keeps the more coercive legal framing.

- **Freedom and repose**
  - French: `votre liberté et votre repos`
  - Fresh translation: `your freedom and your repose`
  - Davidson: `your freedom and your tranquillity`
  - Davidson is close, but `repose` keeps more of the French's static sense of being left untroubled.

- **Master / husband closing opposition**
  - Fresh translation: `forget that I am your master ... remember only that I am your husband`
  - Davidson: `forget that I am your master, and to be remembered only as your husband`
  - Davidson is near here, but the fresh version keeps the sharper contrast between domination and conjugal self-fashioning.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: admonitory, self-serving, and quietly coercive.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the contradiction between Usbek's professed gentleness and his endorsement of the eunuch's authority.
- **Davidson**: reliable and close, though slightly smoother in the manipulative turns.
