## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XXIII**

**Usbek to his friend Ibben, at Smyrna**

### Translation
We arrived in Livorno after forty days at sea. It is a new city, and it stands as a witness to the genius of the dukes of Tuscany, who have made the most flourishing city in Italy out of a marshy village.

Women there enjoy great freedom. They can see men through certain windows called jalousies; they can go out every day, accompanied by a few old women; they wear only one veil. Their brothers-in-law, uncles, and nephews may see them almost without the husband taking offense.

For a Muslim, it is a great spectacle to see a Christian city for the first time. I am not speaking only of the things that first strike every eye, such as the difference in buildings, clothing, and the principal customs. Even in the smallest trifles there is something singular that I feel and do not know how to express.

We leave tomorrow for Marseille; our stay there will not be long. Rica and I intend to go at once to Paris, which is the seat of the empire of Europe. Travelers always seek out the great cities, which are a kind of common fatherland for all foreigners. Farewell. Be persuaded that I shall always love you.

*At Livorno, the 12th day of the moon of Saphar, 1712.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson follows this short travel letter closely. The comparison is mostly about lexical and tonal choices rather than structural divergence, though a few civic terms lose force in English.

### Main differences
- **City vs town**
  - French: `la ville d'Italie la plus florissante`
  - Fresh translation: `the most flourishing city in Italy`
  - Davidson: `the most flourishing town in Italy`
  - Minor, but `ville` is better preserved as `city` in this context.

- **A great spectacle**
  - French: `C'est un grand spectacle`
  - Fresh translation: `it is a great spectacle`
  - Davidson: `a wonderful spectacle`
  - Davidson heightens the phrase slightly.

- **Common fatherland**
  - French: `une espèce de patrie commune`
  - Fresh translation: `a kind of common fatherland`
  - Davidson: `a sort of common country`
  - `patrie` has a stronger civic and affective resonance than `country`.

- **Closing assurance**
  - French: `Sois persuadé que je t'aimerai toujours`
  - Fresh translation: `Be persuaded that I shall always love you`
  - Davidson: `Rest assured that I shall never cease to love you`
  - Davidson is graceful and close, though slightly more expansive.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: observant, curious, and transitional, marking the passage into Europe.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the measured surprise and the civic emphasis of `patrie`.
- **Davidson**: broadly faithful, with mild smoothing and occasional lexical drift.
