## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CLVII**

**Zachi to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
O heaven! a barbarian has outraged me even in the very manner of punishing me! He inflicted on me that punishment whose first effect is to alarm modesty; that punishment which casts one into the deepest humiliation; that punishment which, so to speak, brings one back to childhood.

My soul, at first annihilated by shame, was recovering the sense of itself and beginning to grow indignant, when my cries rang through the vaults of my apartments. They heard me beg mercy from the vilest of all human beings and try to awaken his pity in proportion as he became more inexorable.

Since that time his insolent and servile soul has risen above mine. His presence, his looks, his words — every misery comes to crush me. When I am alone, I at least have the consolation of shedding tears; but when he appears before my eyes, fury seizes me; I find it powerless; and I fall into despair.

The tiger dares tell me that you are the author of all these barbarities. He would take away my love and profane even the feelings of my heart. When he speaks to me the name of the one I love, I no longer know how to complain: I can do nothing but die.

I endured your absence, and I preserved my love by the force of my love. Nights, days, moments — everything was for you. I was proud even of my love itself; and yours made me respected here. But now... No, I can no longer bear the humiliation to which I have fallen. If I am innocent, return to love me; return, if I am guilty, so that I may die at your feet.

*From the seraglio in Ispahan, the 2nd day of the moon of Maharram, 1720.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson captures the letter's structure and major emotions effectively: shame, rage, humiliation, and the plea for Usbek's return. His version is strong and readable, though it sometimes interprets the French more freely than the earlier letters do.

### Main differences
- **Description of the punishment**
  - French: `ce châtiment qui commence par alarmer la pudeur`
  - Fresh translation: `that punishment whose first effect is to alarm modesty`
  - Davidson: `that chastisement, the first effect of which is to shock one's modesty`
  - Davidson is close, though `shock` is a little more abrupt than the French `alarm`.

- **Recovering the self**
  - French: `reprenoit le sentiment d'elle-même`
  - Fresh translation: `was recovering the sense of itself`
  - Davidson: `recovered consciousness`
  - Davidson gives the general idea, but the French is more about recovering inward self-possession than bare consciousness.

- **The eunuch's domination**
  - French: `son âme insolente et servile s'est élevée sur la mienne`
  - Fresh translation: `his insolent and servile soul has risen above mine`
  - Davidson: `his insolent and slavish mind dominates mine`
  - Davidson paraphrases effectively, but loses the image of his degraded soul mounting above hers.

- **Accumulation of suffering**
  - French: `tous les malheurs viennent m'accabler`
  - Fresh translation: `every misery comes to crush me`
  - Davidson: `all horrible things, overwhelm me`
  - Davidson is forceful, but the French's `malheurs` is broader and less rhetorical than `horrible things`.

- **Final humiliation**
  - French: `l'humiliation où je suis descendue`
  - Fresh translation: `the humiliation to which I have fallen`
  - Davidson: `the humiliation which has overtaken me`
  - Davidson makes the humiliation seem more external; the French stresses descent.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: wounded, furious, and painfully self-aware; the letter turns personal shame into accusation.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the bodily humiliation and the sudden swings from fury to collapse.
- **Davidson**: often close, but slightly more polished and interpretive in phrases where the French is stranger or more violent.

## Working conclusion
Letter CLVII is substantially faithful in Davidson, but it shows a recurrent tendency to smooth strong French images into more generalized English:
- inward self-recovery becomes `consciousness`
- the image of the eunuch's soul rising above hers becomes simple domination
- humiliation as descent becomes humiliation that merely `overtakes`
