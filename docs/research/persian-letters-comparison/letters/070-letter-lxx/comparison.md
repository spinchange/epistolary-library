## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXX**

**Zélis to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
Soliman, whom you love, is in despair over an affront he has just received. For the past three months a young scatterbrain named Suphis had been seeking his daughter in marriage. He seemed pleased with the girl's appearance from the reports and description given him by the women who had seen her in childhood. The dowry had been agreed upon, and everything had passed without any incident.

Yesterday, after the first ceremonies, the girl came out on horseback, accompanied by her eunuch and covered, according to custom, from head to foot. But as soon as she arrived before the house of her intended husband, he had the door shut on her, and he swore he would never receive her unless the dowry were increased. The relatives rushed in from both sides to settle the affair; and after a great deal of resistance they brought Soliman to agree to make a small present to his son-in-law.

At last, the wedding ceremonies having been completed, the girl was taken to bed with a good deal of violence. But an hour later that scatterbrain rose in a fury, cut her face in several places, maintained that she was not a virgin, and sent her back to her father. No one could be more struck than he is by that insult. There are people who maintain that the girl is innocent. Fathers are very unfortunate to be exposed to such affronts. If such treatment happened to my daughter, I think I would die of grief. Farewell.

*From the seraglio of Fatmé, the 9th day of the first moon of Gemmadi, 1714.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson reproduces the letter's anecdotal sequence well: negotiated marriage, dowry extortion at the door, consummation-night accusation, and the father's public dishonor.

### Main differences
- **`Étourdi`**
  - Fresh translation: `scatterbrain`
  - Davidson: `giddypate`
  - Davidson's period term is vivid, but it pushes the letter into a more quaint register than the French requires.

- **Reports of the girl's appearance**
  - French: `sur le rapport et la peinture`
  - Fresh translation: `from the reports and description`
  - Davidson: `from the report and description`
  - Davidson is close here.

- **Marriage-bed violence**
  - French: `on conduisit la fille dans le lit avec assez de violence`
  - Fresh translation: `the girl was taken to bed with a good deal of violence`
  - Davidson: `conducted to her husband's bed with sufficient violence`
  - Davidson is accurate, but the fresh version keeps a slightly harsher force.

- **Family honor**
  - French: `On ne peut pas être plus frappé qu'il l'est de cette injure`
  - Fresh translation: `No one could be more struck than he is by that insult`
  - Davidson: `No one could be more afflicted than he is by this injury`
  - Davidson softens the public-shame aspect by turning `injure` toward personal affliction.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: concise, severe, and socially anxious; the scandal matters as much as the suffering.
- **Fresh translation**: tries to keep the letter's hard social logic and its repeated concern with affront and dishonor.
- **Davidson**: faithful in substance, though slightly more novelistic in diction.

Letter LXX is brief but revealing about marriage, dowry, and family honor. Davidson is close overall; the fresh translation mainly restores some of the sharper social language around insult and violence.
