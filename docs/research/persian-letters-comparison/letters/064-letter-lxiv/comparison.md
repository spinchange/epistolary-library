## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXIV**

**The chief of the black eunuchs to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
I am in an embarrassment I cannot express to you, magnificent lord. The seraglio is in dreadful disorder and confusion. War reigns among your wives; your eunuchs are divided; one hears nothing but complaints, murmurs, and reproaches. My remonstrances are despised. Everything seems permitted in this time of license, and I no longer possess in the seraglio anything but a vain title.

There is not one of your wives who does not judge herself above the others by birth, beauty, riches, wit, or your love, and who does not press one or another of those titles in order to claim every preference. At every moment I lose that long patience with which, however, I have had the misfortune to displease them all. My prudence, even my complaisance — a virtue so rare and so foreign to the office I occupy — have been useless.

Would you have me reveal to you, magnificent lord, the cause of all these disorders? It lies wholly in your heart and in the tender regard you have for them. If you did not hold my hand back, if instead of the path of remonstrance you left me that of punishments, if, instead of letting yourself be softened by their complaints and tears, you sent them to weep before me, who am never softened, I would soon train them to the yoke they must bear and weary out their imperious and independent temper.

Taken from the depths of Africa, my homeland, when I was fifteen, I was first sold to a master who had more than twenty wives or concubines. Judging from my grave and taciturn air that I was suited to the seraglio, he ordered that I be completed for that use, and had performed on me an operation painful at first but happy for me in the sequel, because it brought me near the ear and confidence of my masters. I entered that seraglio, which was for me a new world. The first eunuch there, the sternest man I ever saw, governed it with absolute authority. One heard there neither divisions nor quarrels. Profound silence reigned everywhere. All those women were put to bed at the same hour throughout the year and rose at the same hour; they entered the bath in turn and left it at the least sign we made; the rest of the time they were almost always shut up in their rooms. He had one rule: to keep them in great cleanliness, and for that he showed inexpressible attentions. The slightest refusal to obey was punished without mercy. I am a slave, he used to say, but the slave of a man who is your master and mine; and I use on you the power he has given me. It is he who chastises you, not I, who only lend my hand. These women never entered my master's room unless they had been called. They received that favor with joy and saw themselves deprived of it without complaint. I myself, who was the lowest of the blacks in that tranquil seraglio, was a thousand times more respected than I am in yours, where I command them all.

As soon as that great eunuch recognized my abilities, he turned his eyes toward me. He spoke of me to my master as a man capable of working according to his views and succeeding him in the office he held. He was not surprised by my youth; he thought my attentiveness would stand in place of experience. What shall I say? I advanced so far in his confidence that he no longer hesitated to entrust me with the keys of the terrible places he had guarded for so long. Under that great master I learned the difficult art of command and formed myself in the maxims of an inflexible government. Under him I studied the heart of women. He taught me to profit from their weaknesses and not be astonished by their high airs. Often he even liked to make me exercise those methods and drive them to the last entrenchment of obedience; afterward he would gradually bring them back and wanted me myself to appear to bend for a time. But one should have seen him in those moments when he found them close to despair, wavering between prayers and reproaches. He sustained their tears without being moved. That, he would say with satisfaction, is how women must be governed. Their number does not embarrass me. I would govern in the same way all the women of our great monarch. How can a man hope to captivate their hearts if his faithful eunuchs have not begun by subduing their minds?

He had not firmness alone but penetration as well. He read their thoughts and their dissimulations. Their studied gestures and feigned faces hid nothing from him. He knew their most secret actions and words; he used some to discover the others, and he took pleasure in rewarding the smallest confidence. Since they approached their husband only when they were summoned, the eunuch called whom he wished and directed his master's eyes toward those he had in mind; that distinction was the reward for some secret revealed. He had persuaded his master that good order required the choice to be left to him, so as to give him greater authority. That, magnificent lord, is how one governed in a seraglio that was, I believe, the best regulated in Persia.

Leave my hands free. Permit me to make myself obeyed. Eight days will restore order in the bosom of confusion. Your glory demands it, and your safety requires it.

*From your seraglio at Isfahan, the 9th day of the moon of Rebiab I, 1714.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson reproduces the letter's broad structure and argument very well: present disorder in the seraglio, blame laid on Usbek's softness, the eunuch's self-justifying memoir of training under a harsher predecessor, and the final request for unrestricted punitive power.

### Main differences
- **Physical metaphor of restraint**
  - French: `si tu ne me retenois par la main`
  - Fresh translation: `If you did not hold my hand back`
  - Davidson: `If you did not withhold my hand`
  - Davidson is close, but the fresh version keeps the more tactile image of direct restraint.

- **Cold euphemism for mutilation**
  - French: `qu'on achevât de me rendre tel`
  - Fresh translation: `that I be completed for that use`
  - Davidson: `that I should be prepared for it`
  - Davidson explains the phrase a little more openly; the French is chilling because it stays euphemistic.

- **`Complaisance`**
  - Fresh translation: `complaisance`
  - Davidson: `kindness`
  - `Kindness` is readable, but the French suggests a more strategic accommodatingness rather than simple benevolence.

- **Military image of obedience**
  - French: `le dernier retranchement de l'obéissance`
  - Fresh translation: `the last entrenchment of obedience`
  - Davidson: `the very last verge`
  - Davidson loses the siege metaphor that sharpens the eunuch's theory of domination.

- **Mind before heart**
  - French: `soumettre leur esprit`
  - Fresh translation: `subduing their minds`
  - Davidson: `breaking their spirits`
  - Davidson heightens the violence. The French is already severe, but more specifically disciplinary and administrative.

- **Studied exterior vs inward reading**
  - French: `leurs gestes étudiés, leur visage feint`
  - Fresh translation: `Their studied gestures and feigned faces`
  - Davidson: `Their studied gestures, their made-up looks`
  - Davidson is effective, but slightly more idiomatic and less exact.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: bureaucratic, self-glorifying, and frighteningly lucid about domination.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the memoir-like shape while preserving the euphemistic cruelty of the eunuch's ideology.
- **Davidson**: strong and readable, though sometimes more dramatic where the French is icily administrative.

## Working conclusion
Letter LXIV is a key statement of power inside the seraglio. Davidson is broadly faithful, but the fresh translation preserves more of the French's mix of euphemism, bureaucratic control, and military metaphor.
