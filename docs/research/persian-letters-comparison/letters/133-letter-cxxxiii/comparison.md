## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXXXIII**

**Rica to \*\*\***

### Translation
The other day I went to see a great library in a convent of dervishes, who are something like its custodians, but who are obliged to let everyone in at certain hours.

As I entered, I saw a grave man walking in the midst of an innumerable quantity of volumes surrounding him. I went up to him and asked him to tell me what some of those books were that I saw better bound than the others. 'Sir,' he said to me, 'I live here in a foreign land, and I know no one in it. A great many people ask me questions of that sort; but you can plainly see that I am not going to read all those books to satisfy them. But I have my librarian, who will satisfy you, for he occupies himself night and day deciphering everything you see there. He is a man good for nothing, and a great burden to us, because he does no work for the convent. But I hear the refectory bell ringing. Those who, like me, are at the head of a community must be the first at all its exercises.' As he said this, the monk pushed me out, shut the door, and, as if he had flown, disappeared before my eyes.

*Paris, the 21st day of the moon of Rhamazan, 1719.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson gives a faithful miniature of the joke: the apparent master of the library knows nothing about the books, dismisses inquiry, and treats the actual scholar as a useless dependent because his work does not directly serve convent routine.

### Main differences
- **`comme les dépositaires`**
  - Fresh translation uses `something like its custodians`, which preserves the sense that the dervishes are keepers rather than readers.
  - Davidson's `to whose care it has been entrusted` is close, but less dry.

- **`déchiffrer`**
  - Fresh translation keeps `deciphering`.
  - Davidson effectively weakens the joke by making the librarian simply a reader or explainer; the French suggests laborious scholarly decoding.

- **`n'est bon à rien`**
  - Fresh translation keeps the blunt insult: `good for nothing`.
  - Davidson does this too, and it is one of the best touches in the English version.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: very short, very dry, and perfectly pointed.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the institutional satire by contrasting ceremonial authority with actual learning.
- **Davidson**: close and effective, with only a little smoothing.

Letter CXXXIII needs less correction than many nearby letters. The fresh draft mainly restores the sharpness of `deciphering` and the bureaucratic contempt directed at the only person actually using the library.
