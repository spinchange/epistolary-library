## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXL**

**Rica to Usbek, at \*\*\***

### Translation
The Parliament of Paris has just been relegated to a little town called Pontoise. The council sent it a declaration to register or approve, one that dishonors it; and it registered it in a manner that dishonors the council.

Several parliaments of the kingdom are being threatened with similar treatment.

These bodies are always odious: they approach kings only to tell them sad truths; and while a crowd of courtiers endlessly portray to them a people made happy under their government, they come to give the lie to flattery and to bring to the foot of the throne the groans and tears of which they are the depositaries.

It is a heavy burden, my dear Usbek, this burden of truth, when one must carry it to princes: they ought to reflect that those who do so are constrained to it, and that they would never resolve to take steps so sad and so painful for those who take them, if they were not forced to do so by duty, by respect, and even by love.

*Paris, the 21st day of the moon of Gemmadi I, 1720.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson gives a very close rendering of this brief political letter. He preserves the central contrast between flattering courtiers and hated magistrates who must carry unwelcome truth to the throne.

### Main differences
- **`relégué`**
  - Fresh translation: `relegated`
  - Davidson: `banished`
  - `Banished` is stronger and more dramatic. The French is punitive, but slightly more administrative in tone.

- **`de tristes vérités`**
  - Fresh translation: `sad truths`
  - Davidson: `disagreeable truths`
  - Davidson captures the courtly effect, but `tristes` keeps more of the sorrow and gravity of the French.

- **Depositaries of grief**
  - French: `les gémissements et les larmes dont elles sont dépositaires`
  - Fresh translation preserves the image of entrusted grief.
  - Davidson's `tearful complaints committed to them` is good, but less concrete and less solemn.

- **`démarches`**
  - Fresh translation: `steps`
  - Davidson: `a course`
  - Davidson generalizes slightly. The French suggests specific acts of remonstrance and procedure.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: compressed, grave, and political; the rhetoric is brief but carefully weighted.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the solemnity and the burden imagery central to the closing sentence.
- **Davidson**: accurate and dignified, though a little more generalized in diction.
