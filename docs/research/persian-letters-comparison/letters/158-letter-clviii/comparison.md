## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CLVIII**

**Zelis to Usbek, at Paris**

### Translation
A thousand leagues away from me, you judge me guilty; a thousand leagues away from me, you punish me.

When a barbarous eunuch lays his vile hands on me, he acts on your orders: it is the tyrant who outrages me, not the one who carries out the tyranny.

You may, if it pleases you, redouble your ill-treatment. My heart has been calm since it can no longer love you. Your soul is degrading itself, and you are becoming cruel. Be sure that you are not happy. Farewell.

*From the seraglio in Ispahan, the 2nd day of the moon of Maharram, 1720.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson reproduces the basic structure and bitterness of this very short letter well. The first two sentences are close, compact, and forceful.

### Main differences
- **Instrument of tyranny**
  - French: `c'est le tyran qui m'outrage, et non pas celui qui exerce la tyrannie`
  - Fresh translation: `it is the tyrant who outrages me, not the one who carries out the tyranny`
  - Davidson: `it is the tyrant who outrages me, and not the instrument of his tyranny`
  - Davidson is interpretive rather than literal here, but the meaning is substantially preserved.

- **Ill-treatment**
  - French: `redoubler vos mauvais traitements`
  - Fresh translation: `redouble your ill-treatment`
  - Davidson: `redouble your cruel treatment`
  - Davidson intensifies slightly with `cruel`, though the context certainly allows it.

- **State of the heart**
  - French: `Mon coeur est tranquille`
  - Fresh translation: `My heart has been calm`
  - Davidson: `My heart is at peace`
  - This is a minor stylistic difference; Davidson is slightly more elevated.

- **The crucial closing sentence**
  - French: `Soyez sûr que vous n'êtes point heureux`
  - Fresh translation: `Be sure that you are not happy`
  - Davidson: `Rest assured that you are not beloved`
  - This is the major discrepancy in the letter. The French speaks about Usbek's unhappiness, not about whether he is loved.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: cold, final, and devastatingly concise; Zelis speaks without pleading.
- **Fresh translation**: aims to keep the short strokes and the moral reversal by which Usbek's power becomes evidence of his degradation.
- **Davidson**: very effective in the first two-thirds, but the final sentence changes the point of the letter in an important way.

### OCR / cleanup notes
- The clean English text for this letter is short and clear.
- No significant OCR problem appears; the main issue is the genuine translation difference in the last sentence.

## Working conclusion
Letter CLVIII contains one of the clearest substantive divergences in this batch. Davidson is close almost throughout, but his final line shifts the meaning from:
- `you are not happy`

to:
- `you are not beloved`

That change turns a judgment on Usbek's inner condition into a judgment on how others feel about him.
