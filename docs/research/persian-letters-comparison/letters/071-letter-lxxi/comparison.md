## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXXI**

**Usbek to Zélis**

### Translation
I pity Soliman, especially because the evil is without remedy and his son-in-law has done no more than make use of the liberty granted by the law. I find that law very harsh, exposing in that way the honor of a family to the caprice of a madman. People may say all they like that there are certain signs by which the truth can be known; it is an old error from which we among ourselves have now withdrawn, and our physicians give invincible reasons for the uncertainty of those proofs. Even the Christians regard them as chimerical, although they are clearly established by their sacred books and their ancient legislator made the innocence or condemnation of all girls depend upon them.

I learn with pleasure what care you are taking over your daughter's education. God grant that her husband may find her as beautiful and as pure as Fatima; that she may have ten eunuchs to guard her; that she may be the honor and ornament of the seraglio for which she is destined; that nothing but gilded ceilings may be above her head and that she may walk only on superb carpets; and, to crown these wishes, may my eyes see her in all her glory!

*At Paris, the 5th day of the moon of Chalval, 1714.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson conveys the structure of the letter cleanly: sympathy for Soliman, skepticism about proofs of virginity, and a ceremonially grand wish for the daughter's future marriage.

### Main differences
- **Caprice of a madman**
  - French: `aux caprices d'un fou`
  - Fresh translation: `to the caprice of a madman`
  - Davidson: `to the caprice of a fool`
  - Davidson is close, though `fou` is a little harsher than `fool` in this context.

- **Chimerical proofs**
  - French: `les regardent comme chimériques`
  - Fresh translation: `regard them as chimerical`
  - Davidson: `regard them as imaginary`
  - `Imaginary` is serviceable, but `chimériques` keeps more of the language of illusion and error.

- **All girls / all daughters**
  - French: `de toutes les filles`
  - Fresh translation: `of all girls`
  - Davidson: `of all their daughters`
  - Davidson domesticates the phrase slightly.

- **Date discrepancy**
  - French date: `le 5 de la lune de Chalval`
  - Fresh translation: `the 5th day of the moon of Chalval`
  - Davidson: `the 3rd of the moon of Chalval`
  - This appears to be a real textual discrepancy worth flagging.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: coolly juridical in the first paragraph, then ceremonially expansive in the second.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the legal skepticism and the almost ironic magnificence of the closing wishes.
- **Davidson**: quite close in tone, though the date drift is significant.

## Working conclusion
Letter LXXI is short and mostly straightforward, but it contains one notable factual difference: Davidson's date reads `3rd` where the French gives `5th`. Otherwise his version is close, with only minor tonal smoothing.
