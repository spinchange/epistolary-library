## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XIX**

**Usbek to his friend Rustan, in Isfahan**

### Translation
We stayed only eight days in Tokat. After thirty-five days on the road, we arrived in Smyrna.

From Tokat to Smyrna one does not find a single town worth naming. I have seen with astonishment the weakness of the Ottoman empire. That sick body supports itself not by a gentle and temperate regimen, but by violent remedies that exhaust and undermine it without ceasing.

The pashas, who obtain their offices only by force of money, enter their provinces ruined and ravage them like lands of conquest. An insolent militia is subject only to its own caprices. The fortresses are dismantled, the towns deserted, the countryside laid waste, cultivation of the land and commerce wholly abandoned.

Impunity reigns in that severe government. The Christians who cultivate the soil, and the Jews who collect the taxes, are exposed to a thousand acts of violence.

The ownership of land is uncertain, and consequently the zeal to improve it is weakened. No title and no possession have any force against the caprice of those who govern.

These barbarians have so completely abandoned the arts that they have neglected even the military art. While the nations of Europe refine themselves every day, they remain in their former ignorance and think of adopting new inventions only after those inventions have already been used against them a thousand times.

They have no experience at sea, no skill in seamanship. People say that a handful of Christians come out from a rock and make all the Ottomans sweat and weary their empire.

Incapable of carrying on trade themselves, they are almost pained that Europeans, always industrious and enterprising, come to conduct it. They think they are doing those foreigners a favor by permitting them to enrich them.

In all that vast extent of country I have crossed, I found only Smyrna that can be regarded as a rich and powerful city. It is Europeans who make it so, and it is not the Turks' fault that it does not resemble all the others.

There, dear Rustan, is a just idea of that empire, which, before two centuries pass, will be the stage for the triumphs of some conqueror.

*At Smyrna, the 2nd day of the moon of Rhamazan, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson gives a strong and readable version of Usbek's diagnosis of Ottoman weakness. He follows the letter's political sequence closely, but he occasionally replaces the French's sharper medical and commercial terms with smoother historical prose.

### Main differences
- **Medical `regimen` vs `diet`**
  - French: `un régime doux et tempéré`
  - Fresh translation: `a gentle and temperate regimen`
  - Davidson: `a plain and temperate diet`
  - `regimen` better preserves the medical-political metaphor of the sentence.

- **Lands of conquest**
  - French: `comme des pays de conquête`
  - Fresh translation: `like lands of conquest`
  - Davidson: `like conquered countries`
  - Davidson is accurate, though less forceful in rhythm and image.

- **A rock, not necessarily a barren rock**
  - French: `sortis d'un rocher`
  - Fresh translation: `come out from a rock`
  - Davidson: `descending from a barren rock`
  - `barren` is an addition not present in the French.

- **Who gets enriched**
  - French: `permettre qu'ils les enrichissent`
  - Fresh translation: `permitting them to enrich them`
  - Davidson: `permitting them to enrich themselves`
  - This is the most important discrepancy in the letter: Davidson shifts the object of enrichment from the Turks to the Europeans.

- **Triumphs vs exploits**
  - French: `le théâtre des triomphes de quelque conquérant`
  - Fresh translation: `the stage for the triumphs of some conqueror`
  - Davidson: `the scene of some conqueror's exploits`
  - Davidson remains close, but `triomphes` carries a stronger ceremonial and imperial charge.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: brisk, political, contemptuous, and sharply diagnostic.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the compression and the medical metaphor at the center.
- **Davidson**: generally faithful, but somewhat smoother and more historical in register.

## Working conclusion
Letter XIX is a solid comparison case for Montesquieu's political prose. Davidson transmits the argument well, but the commercial clause about Europeans enriching the Turks is an important place where he materially alters the sentence's direction.
