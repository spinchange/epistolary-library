## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter LXXII**

**Rica to Usbek, at \*\*\*.**

### Translation
The other day I found myself in a company where I saw a man very pleased with himself. In a quarter of an hour he decided three questions of morality, four historical problems, and five points of physics. I have never seen a more universal decision-maker: his mind was never suspended by the slightest doubt. People left the sciences and began talking about current news: he pronounced on the current news as well. I wanted to catch him out, and I said to myself: I must put myself on my own ground; I shall take refuge in my own country. I spoke to him about Persia; but scarcely had I said four words when he gave me two flat contradictions, based on the authority of Messrs. Tavernier and Chardin. Good God, I said to myself, what kind of man is this? In a moment he will know the streets of Isfahan better than I do! My course was soon decided: I kept silent, I let him talk, and he is still deciding.

*At Paris, the 8th day of the moon of Zilcadé, 1715.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson keeps the joke of the letter intact: Rica meets a man who issues judgments on everything, and even presumed expertise about Persia collapses before secondhand travel writing.

### Main differences
- **`Décisionnaire`**
  - Fresh translation: `decision-maker`
  - Davidson: `decider`
  - Davidson is punchier, but the French neologism has a slightly more satirical bureaucratic flavor.

- **Mind suspended by doubt**
  - French: `Son esprit ne fut jamais suspendu par le moindre doute`
  - Fresh translation stays close to the image of thought never once being halted.
  - Davidson's `his mind was not once troubled` is good, though it weakens the mechanical metaphor of suspension.

- **`Deux démentis`**
  - Fresh translation: `two flat contradictions`
  - Davidson: `he contradicted me twice`
  - Davidson gives the sense, but the noun `démentis` is a little more forceful.

- **Ongoing habit of deciding**
  - French: `et il décide encore`
  - Fresh translation: `and he is still deciding`
  - Davidson: `and he is still laying down the law`
  - Davidson sharpens the satire by paraphrase; the French is simpler and drier.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: quick, amused, and mercilessly concise.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the speed of the anecdote and the repeated verbal pattern of deciding everything.
- **Davidson**: lively and funny, though a little more expansive in idiom.
