## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XVI**

**Usbek to Mollak Méhémet Ali, guardian of the three tombs, at Qom**

### Translation
Why do you live among tombs, divine mollak? You are much better made for the dwelling of the stars. No doubt you hide yourself for fear of obscuring the sun. You have no spots like that star, but like it you cover yourself with clouds.

Your knowledge is an abyss deeper than the ocean; your mind is sharper than Zulfiqar, Ali's two-pointed sword. You know what passes in the nine choirs of the celestial powers. You read the Koran upon the breast of our divine Prophet; and when you find some obscure passage, an angel, by his order, spreads his rapid wings and descends from the throne to reveal its secret to you.

Through you I might have intimate correspondence with the seraphim. For after all, thirteenth imam, are you not the center where heaven and earth meet, and the point of communication between the abyss and the empyrean?

I am in the midst of a profane people. Allow me to purify myself with you. Suffer me to turn my face toward the sacred places you inhabit. Distinguish me from the wicked, as at the break of dawn one distinguishes the white thread from the black thread. Help me with your counsels. Take care of my soul. Intoxicate it with the spirit of the prophets. Nourish it with the knowledge of paradise, and allow me to lay its wounds at your feet. Address your sacred letters to Erzurum, where I shall remain some months.

*From Erzurum, the 11th day of the second moon of Gemmadi, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the basic satirical movement of the letter, but the English clean text is badly contaminated by intrusive footnotes. Even where the sense survives, he often paraphrases the French's images into a more devotional and Victorian idiom.

### Main differences
- **Cloud imagery**
  - French: `comme lui, tu te couvres de nuages`
  - Fresh translation: `like it you cover yourself with clouds`
  - Davidson: `you resemble him in your cloudy concealment`
  - Davidson keeps the idea but turns a plain image into a decorative abstraction.

- **Zulfiqar passage broken by footnotes**
  - French: `ton esprit est plus perçant que Zufagar`
  - Fresh translation: `your mind is sharper than Zulfiqar`
  - Davidson's sentence is interrupted by a long editorial note inside the clean text.
  - This is more than a style issue: the OCR/edition state actively disrupts comparison in the middle of the paragraph.

- **Koran on the Prophet's breast**
  - French: `tu lis l'Alcoran sur la poitrine de notre divin prophète`
  - Fresh translation keeps the startling bodily image.
  - Davidson retains it in principle, but the sentence is broken apart by note debris and loses rhetorical continuity.

- **White thread / black thread**
  - French: `le filet blanc d'avec le filet noir`
  - Fresh translation: `the white thread from the black thread`
  - Davidson: `as one distinguishes night from day`
  - This is a real substitution, not just stylistic smoothing.

- **Soul care and nourishment**
  - French: `prends soin de mon âme; enivre-la ... nourris-la`
  - Fresh translation keeps the imperative sequence and bodily metaphors.
  - Davidson: `be my soul's guardian; feed me with divine knowledge`
  - Davidson compresses the parallel structure and partially shifts the object from `my soul` to `me`.

- **Duration of stay**
  - French: `où je resterai quelques mois`
  - Fresh translation: `where I shall remain some months`
  - Davidson: `where I shall stay for a month or two`
  - Davidson narrows an indefinite phrase into a more specific estimate.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: extravagantly deferential on the surface, with satire emerging through inflation.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the straight-faced excess without reducing it to piety.
- **Davidson**: often understandable, but both OCR contamination and stylistic smoothing make the tone more reverent than the French.

### Notable OCR / edition notes
- The English clean text is heavily contaminated by footnotes, especially in the Zulfiqar sentence and the `thirteenth imam` passage.
- The opening title is split across lines and includes note markers (`the Three Tombs 1 at Koum`).
- The phrase `night from day \ %` shows visible OCR/editorial corruption.

## Working conclusion
Letter XVI is one of the clearest cases where edition and OCR problems interfere with literary comparison. Even so, Davidson can still be seen smoothing important images, especially the Quranic `white thread` / `black thread` line and the clustered imperatives about purifying and nourishing the soul.
