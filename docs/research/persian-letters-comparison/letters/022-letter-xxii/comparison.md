## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter XXII**

**Jaron to the chief eunuch**

### Translation
As Usbek moves farther from the seraglio, he turns his head back toward his sacred wives; he sighs, he sheds tears, his grief grows sharper, his suspicions stronger. He wants to increase the number of their guardians. He is going to send me back, along with all the black eunuchs accompanying him. He no longer fears for himself: he fears for what is a thousand times dearer to him than himself.

So I am going to live under your laws and share your cares. Great God, how many things it takes to make a single man happy!

Nature seemed to have placed women in dependence and then withdrawn them from it. Disorder arose between the two sexes because their rights were reciprocal. We have entered into the plan of a new harmony: between women and us we have placed hatred, and between men and women, love.

My brow will become severe. I shall let dark looks fall. Joy will flee my lips. The exterior will be calm and the spirit anxious. I shall not wait for the wrinkles of old age to display its sorrows.

I would have taken pleasure in following my master into the West; but my will is his property. He wants me to guard his wives; I shall guard them faithfully. I know how I ought to behave toward that sex which, when not allowed to be vain, begins to grow proud, and which is less easy to humble than to annihilate. I place myself beneath your gaze.

*From Smyrna, the 12th day of the moon of Zilcadé, 1711.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson preserves the situation and the self-fashioning of Jaron as an instrument of control. The main differences are tonal and lexical: the French is colder, more schematic, and more brutal in its account of sex and discipline.

### Main differences
- **Turning his head back**
  - French: `il tourne sa tête vers ses femmes sacrées`
  - Fresh translation: `he turns his head back toward his sacred wives`
  - Davidson: `he thinks of these devoted women`
  - Davidson drops the physical image and weakens the ceremonial tone of `sacred wives`.

- **Constructed `new harmony`**
  - French: `nous avons mis entre les femmes et nous la haine`
  - Fresh translation: `between women and us we have placed hatred`
  - Davidson: `hatred is the link between women and us poor eunuchs`
  - Davidson smooths the active, engineered structure of the French.

- **Humble vs annihilate**
  - French: `moins aisé d'humilier que d'anéantir`
  - Fresh translation: `less easy to humble than to annihilate`
  - Davidson: `easier to break than to bend`
  - This is the sharpest divergence in the letter: Davidson substitutes a proverb-like contrast for a much harsher original.

- **Falling under your gaze**
  - French: `Je tombe sous tes regards`
  - Fresh translation: `I place myself beneath your gaze`
  - Davidson: `I prostrate myself before you`
  - Davidson makes the close more ceremonially submissive than the French requires.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: cold, ideological, and theatrically disciplinary.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps Jaron's programmatic voice and the cruelty embedded in its abstractions.
- **Davidson**: readable and coherent, but distinctly less brutal at key points.

## Working conclusion
Letter XXII is short but revealing. Davidson captures the setup, but one of the letter's most important formulations — `humilier` versus `anéantir` — is substantially softened, which dulls Jaron's ideological violence.
