## Fresh translation draft

### Heading
**Letter CXXIV**

**Usbek to Mollak Méhémet Ali, guardian of the three tombs, at Qom**

### Translation
What use are the fasts of the imams to us, and the hair shirts of the mollaks? God's hand has twice fallen heavily on the children of the Law; the sun grows dark, and seems to shine only upon their defeats; their armies gather, and are scattered like dust.

The empire of the Osmanlis is shaken by the two greatest disasters it has ever suffered: a Christian mufti supports it only with difficulty; the grand vizier of Germany is the scourge of God, sent to chastise the followers of Omar; everywhere he carries the wrath of heaven, angered by their rebellion and perfidy.

Holy spirit of the imams, you weep night and day over the children of the Prophet, whom detestable Omar led astray; your very bowels are stirred at the sight of their misfortunes; you desire their conversion, not their destruction; you would like to see them gathered again beneath Ali's banner by the tears of the saints, and not scattered through mountains and deserts by the terror of the infidels.

*Paris, the 1st day of the moon of Chalval, 1718.*

## Comparison against Davidson

### Davidson text summary
Davidson is broadly close here. He keeps the polemical structure of the letter: Shiite devotion, Ottoman defeat, and the recasting of European commanders in Islamic political language.

### Main differences
- **`les enfants de la loi`**
  - Fresh translation keeps `the children of the Law`, which preserves the elevated sectarian register.
  - Davidson's `children of the law` is substantially accurate, though slightly less emphatic in rhythm.

- **European offices recoded in Muslim terms**
  - French: `un moufti chrétien` / `le grand vizir d'Allemagne`
  - Fresh translation keeps the satirical transposition exactly.
  - Davidson also keeps it, which is one of the strengths of his version.

- **`tes entrailles s'émeuvent`**
  - Fresh translation retains the bodily intensity with `your very bowels are stirred`.
  - Davidson's biblical diction is effective, but the fresh draft tries to keep the phrase vivid without making the whole passage sound uniformly archaic.

- **Ali's banner**
  - The French closing movement is devotional and political at once: reunion under Ali rather than dispersion by infidel terror.
  - Davidson is close in sense, though the fresh draft keeps the contrast between conversion and military rout slightly sharper.

### Tone comparison
- **French original**: brief, lamenting, and fiercely sectarian in its rhetoric.
- **Fresh translation**: keeps the prayer-like cadence while preserving the political joke of translating European events into Shiite terms.
- **Davidson**: generally faithful, but more steeped in archaizing biblical English.
